# INDEX: AP European History Unit 9 (Cold War and Contemporary Europe, c. 1914 to present) sources

Bank version 2026-11-09g.

Copyright rule for this unit (Units 8 and 9): no copyrighted post-1930 private text is used (no memoirs, novels, newspaper articles, or modern historians). Every source has its own license basis, given in the table below and argued in its own file:
- **UK Parliament Hansard**, reused under the Open Parliament Licence v3.0, with the credit "Contains Parliamentary information licensed under the Open Parliament Licence v3.0" on every Hansard source (X2, X3 Source 1, P3, P9, P16). The OPL page answered HTTP 403 to curl and the Wayback Machine refused the connection, so no copy of the licence is in raw/web/; the Hansard pages saved in raw/hansard/ carry "© UK Parliament" and a link to the copyright page.
- **Official EU documents** from EUR-Lex, reused under the Commission's reuse policy (Decision 2011/833/EU, saved as raw/eu/eurlex_32011D0833.*; EUR-Lex and europa.eu legal notices saved in raw/web/), credited "© European Union … (CC BY 4.0)" (X3 Source 2, X8, P8, P10, P11, P12, P15). The official College Board practice exam credits EU text the same way.
- **UN official records and documents** (X5, X7, P1, P2, P6, P14), which UN administrative instruction ST/AI/189/Add.9/Rev.2, para. 2, leaves in the public domain (saved as raw/web/un_ST_AI_189_Add.9_Rev.2.pdf). P13 is a treaty printed in the UN Treaty Series; its basis is that the text is an official text of the signatory states (German UrhG § 5, saved in raw/web/; Russian Civil Code art. 1259(6); an authentic English text adopted by the U.S. and other governments). Caveats raised by the independent check (the instruction's "experimental" clause; the underlying texts being official communications of states, with the Hungarian statute cited from memory) are recorded in X5.1, X5.2, P6.1 and P6.2 (and apply to P1, Gorbachev's 1988 address in a UN interpretation from Russian); P13.md adds the U.S. government-edicts doctrine and notes that Russian Civil Code art. 1259(6) is cited but not saved.
- **Works of the U.S. government** (P4, an essay by State Department historians; X1, the Potsdam Protocol as printed in Foreign Relations of the United States, 1960; 17 U.S.C. § 105; the Protocol itself is an official agreement of the three governments). The State Department's copyright notice ("Unless a copyright is indicated, information on State Department websites is in the public domain", archived copy of state.gov/misc/87529.htm, 2013) and the Milestones front page ("drafted for the Office of the Historian website by many historians") are saved in raw/web/. No U.S. government speech is used.
- **CC BY 4.0 data** (X6, Maddison Project Database 2023; P7, World Bank WDI).
- **CC BY-SA 3.0 DE images** from the German Federal Archives (X4, P5), license confirmed with the Commons API (raw/commons/*_api.json) and page wikitext. Both credit lines give the photographer (or "Photographer unknown"), the archive ID, the license and its URL (https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/3.0/de/).

Every text excerpt was copied from a raw download saved in sources/raw/ and machine-checked against it with tools/check_excerpts.py: every stretch between ellipses or brackets had to occur in the raw text once both were reduced to lowercase letters (case, punctuation, digits, whitespace and line-end hyphens ignored). Where the raw text is an OCR layer that interleaves columns or garbles words (S/PV.1441, A/3251, A/3311), the passages used were read by eye from the page images and saved as separate transcriptions in raw/transcribed/, and the check was run against those. For A/43/PV.72 (P1), whose own text layer is garbled, the transcription in raw/transcribed/ was built by script from an OCR of the page images and corrected word by word against the page images; a second OCR run and the PDF's own text layer were compared with it (see P1.md). A/RES/1514(XV) (P2) has a good text layer, extracted column by column (raw/un/A_RES_1514_XV_columns.txt). Five stretches still differ from the raw text, and each was read by hand: P1 (the typescript's misspelling "extrememly," printed as "extremely" and disclosed in the credit line), P2 (the text layer's OCR slip "statt1s"; the page image reads "status"), P6 Source 2 (the telegram's capitals and STOPs, normalized and disclosed in the credit line), P9 (the online Hansard's "county," corrected to "country" and disclosed), and P14 (the text layer's "Recognising"; the page image reads "Recognizing"). Numbers are not covered by the letter-only check; every number in the excerpts was compared with the raw text or page image by eye (for P1 and P2 a second check kept letters and digits, with the two disclosed corrections reverted: all 16 stretches match). Table values were read from the downloaded datasets by script and are listed in X6.md and P7.md. Each source file (sources/<ID>.md) prints the excerpt as it appears in the bank, the raw region it was cut from, and notes on every cut and correction.

Balance of voices (22 text sources):
- British (Hansard): Attlee, Thatcher, Bevan, Howe, Glenarthur, Mowlam (five sets).
- The three wartime Allies together: the Potsdam Protocol (X1).
- European Community / Union institutions: the Maastricht Treaty, the Charter, the Court of Justice, the 1973 environment programme, the 1986 declaration, the 1989 directive, the 1998 euro regulation.
- Central and Eastern Europe and the Soviet Union: Gorbachev (Soviet leader, 1988), Czechoslovakia's Communist Party leaders, the Soviet UN representative, Nagy and Kádár (Hungary), the leaders of Belarus, Russia and Ukraine, and the Two Plus Four Treaty (both German states, France, the USSR, Britain, the U.S.).
- The UN General Assembly's 1960 declaration against colonialism, proposed by 43 African and Asian states (a non-European voice on empire and self-determination); the UN Secretary-General (Annan, a later writer on Bosnia); U.S. government historians (one essay). Images are a West German government photograph and an East German photo-agency photograph.

Not used (with the reason):
- The Schuman Declaration (1950): it is a source in the official College Board practice exam used for calibration, so a different EU text was chosen.
- A 1950 Marshall Plan poster on Commons tagged PD-USGov: the designer was a private Dutch artist, so the public-domain basis is doubtful. The East German photo-agency photo (P5) is used instead.
- The Brundtland Report (UN document A/42/427, 1987): its text was also published commercially under the World Commission's own copyright, so the 1973 EC environment programme (P11) is used instead.
- The Basic Law of 1949 in a U.S. State Department translation: the only scans found are lending-library copies whose text cannot be downloaded.
- The Treaty of Rome (1957) and the Single European Act (1986): English texts were not retrieved for use in this bank (the saved EUR-Lex page for the 1957 treaty, eu/eurlex_11957E_TXT.*, is empty; EUR-Lex does carry an English text of the Single European Act, which was not downloaded). Corrected in v2026-11-09d; the earlier note said EUR-Lex had no English text of either.
- The Good Friday Agreement in the UN Treaty Series: the volume link could not be found; Mowlam's Commons statement (P16) is used instead.
- David Steel's 1966 abortion bill speech (Hansard) was downloaded but not used; the Warnock debate (P9) covers medical technology and moral debate.
