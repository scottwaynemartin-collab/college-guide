# INDEX: AP European History Unit 2 (Age of Reformation, c. 1450–c. 1648) sources

Every text excerpt was copied from raw text downloaded with curl and saved in sources/raw/ (a second scan of McGlothlin is in raw/alt/), then machine-checked stretch by stretch against the raw text with a throwaway script: each stretch between ellipses, brackets, or footnote marks had to occur in the raw text after case, punctuation, whitespace, and line-end hyphens were normalized. Stretches that did not match exactly were read by hand against the raw text and, where the OCR was garbled, against the page image (archive.org IIIF or BookReader page images saved in raw/img/); every mismatch is an OCR slip, a reader's pencil mark, a running head or page break, an interleaved margin note (Robinson and Gee & Hardy print side notes), or a removed footnote mark, and each is listed in its file. Every edition or translation used was published in 1930 or earlier. Quoted words in explanations, hints, and why-not lines were also checked by script against the visible excerpt (0 unmatched). Images are public-domain files on Wikimedia Commons whose license was confirmed with the Commons API (requests spaced out after HTTP 429 responses) and which were downloaded to raw/commons/ and viewed before the captions were written. Chart values were read from the page images, and every row sum and printed total was checked.

Notes:
- Fall 2026 AP European History CED Unit 2 has eight topics (2.1–2.8) and Learning Objectives A–G (2.3 shares LO B with 2.2); see ced_unit2_excerpt.txt. The exam uses eight sets of two to five questions (25 in all); practice uses sixteen sets of three.
- Brackets in excerpts are the editors' own (Robinson, Burr, McGlothlin) unless a file says otherwise; no bank brackets were added. Footnotes marked * are the bank's.
- Weber (X8): the scan used (archive.org protestantethics00webe) is not a 1930 printing. Its imprint page names Charles Scribner's Sons (New York) and George Allen & Unwin (London) and reads 'First published in Great Britain in 1930 / Second impression 1948 / Third impression 1950', printed by Butler and Tanner. An impression reprints the same setting of type, so the text and page numbers are those of the 1930 Parsons translation; the student-facing credit cites the 1930 edition, and the 1930 rule is met.
- Robinson's bracketed date '[1536]' for Calvin's letter is an error for 1535 (the letter is dated at Basel, August 23, 1535); the attribution gives August 1535.
- Images: P3 was downloaded from upload.wikimedia.org. For X6 and P15, upload.wikimedia.org answered HTTP 429 ('robot policy') to this machine for over an hour, so the same 1280px thumbnails were downloaded from thumb.wikimedia.org, the host the Commons API itself returns as 'thumburl' (same path and file); the stimulus src uses the standard upload.wikimedia.org thumbnail URL.
- Kapp (P5) is a German-language source; only figures are used, with the bank's own English labels and note.
- No non-European voice fits this unit's eight topics with a verified pre-1930 English text: Hideyoshi's 1587 edict expelling missionaries was considered, but the only English version found (Murdoch, History of Japan, vol. 2, 1903; raw/murdoch1903v2_historyofjapan02murd_0.txt) is a paraphrase from a French Jesuit account, so it was not used. Multi-date data is supplied by X5 (fifteen decades) and P5 (eleven years).
- Downloaded and considered but not used: W. H. Frere and C. E. Douglas, Puritan Manifestoes (1907) (raw/frere1907_puritanmanifesto00frer.txt; considered for the Admonition to Parliament of 1572, but Puritanism is outside the unit's main focus and Calvinist church discipline is covered by the Geneva pair, P7); Preserved Smith, The Age of the Reformation (1920) (raw/smith1920_agereformation00smitgoog.txt; Weber and Ranke were preferred as historians); Rubens, The Apotheosis of Henry IV (raw/commons/rubens_apotheosis_wga20342_1280.jpg; rejected because the canvas includes a central nude figure).
- Not reused from other Euro units or from aw3 (World Unit 3): Luther's Ninety-five Theses and Address to the Christian Nobility (aw3), Trent Session XXV (aw3; this bank uses Session IV), Busbecq, the Edict of Nantes text, the Peace of Augsburg and Westphalia texts, Bruegel, Richelieu. Montaigne's 'Of Cannibals' is used by ae1 (P12.2) from the same Cotton/Hazlitt edition; this bank uses a different essay, 'Of Liberty of Conscience'.
