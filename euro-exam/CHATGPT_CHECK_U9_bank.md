### Source P1 (questions E9-P01–E9-P03)
> It is also quite clear to us that the principle of freedom of choice is mandatory. Its non-recognition is fraught with extremely grave consequences for world peace. Denying that right to peoples, under whatever pretext or rhetorical guise, jeopardizes even the fragile balance that has been attained. Freedom of choice is a universal principle that should allow of no exceptions. …
> For our society to participate in efforts to implement the plans of perestroika, it had to be democratized in practice. Under the sign of democratization, perestroika has now spread to politics, the economy, intellectual life and ideology.
> We have initiated a radical economic reform. …
> Soviet democracy will be placed on a solid normative base. I am referring, in particular, to laws on the freedom of conscience, glasnost, public associations, organizations, and many others. …
> Today I can report to the General Assembly that the Soviet Union has taken a decision to reduce its armed forces. Within the next two years their numerical strength will be reduced by 500,000 men. … This will be done unilaterally …
> By agreement with our Warsaw Treaty allies we have decided to withdraw, by 1991, six tank divisions from the German Democratic Republic, Czechoslovakia and Hungary and to disband them.
— Mikhail Gorbachev, general secretary of the Communist Party of the Soviet Union and the Soviet head of state, speech to the United Nations General Assembly, New York, December 7, 1988 (Text from United Nations document A/43/PV.72, provisional verbatim record of the General Assembly's 72nd meeting (December 7, 1988), pp. 11, 24–25, 27–28 (UN interpretation from Russian); the typed "extrememly" is corrected to "extremely"; adapted)
> Words to know: mandatory = required; it must be obeyed; fraught with = full of (something bad); pretext or rhetorical guise = false excuse or cover of fine-sounding words; jeopardizes = puts in danger; perestroika = "restructuring": Gorbachev's program to reform the Soviet economy and government; glasnost = "openness": Gorbachev's policy of allowing more free speech and public discussion; normative base = a basis in written laws; unilaterally = by one side alone, without waiting for the other side to act; Warsaw Treaty allies = the communist states of Eastern Europe in the Warsaw Pact, the military alliance led by the Soviet Union

**E9-P01** [Topic 9.7 · LO G · Skill 4.B · easy]  The reforms that Gorbachev describes, such as perestroika and glasnost, were most directly a response to which of the following?
- (A) the Hungarian Revolution of 1956
- (B) the oil price shocks of 1973 and 1979
- (C) long-term Soviet economic stagnation
- (D) the fall of the Berlin Wall in 1989
KEY: C. Explanation: By the late 1970s the Soviet economy grew very slowly. Factories were out of date, and stores often ran short of goods. Gorbachev became leader in 1985. He began perestroika ("restructuring") and glasnost ("openness") to make the system work better.
  - why not A: The Hungarian Revolution came in 1956, more than thirty years before this speech, and Soviet troops crushed it. Gorbachev's reforms answered problems inside the Soviet Union in the 1970s and 1980s.
  - why not B: The oil price rises of 1973 and 1979 hurt Western Europe. The Soviet Union sold oil abroad, so for a time it earned more money.
  - why not D: The Berlin Wall fell in November 1989, almost a year after this speech.
  - hint: Which choice describes a problem at home that Gorbachev's reforms were meant to fix?

**E9-P02** [Topic 9.4 · LO D · Skill 3.D · medium]  Which of the following later developments would best support the argument that Gorbachev put his principle of "freedom of choice" into practice?
- (A) Soviet troops attacked independence protesters in Lithuania in 1991.
- (B) The Soviet Union let Solidarity lead Poland's government in 1989.
- (C) China's army crushed student protests in Tiananmen Square in 1989.
- (D) Yugoslavia broke apart in a series of wars beginning in 1991.
KEY: B. Explanation: In June 1989 Poland held partly free elections. Solidarity won almost every seat it was allowed to run for. Gorbachev did not send troops. In August 1989 Tadeusz Mazowiecki of Solidarity became prime minister.
  - why not A: Using troops against Lithuanians who wanted independence went against freedom of choice. It would challenge the argument, not support it.
  - why not C: China was not under Soviet control. What China's leaders did tells us nothing about Soviet policy.
  - why not D: Yugoslavia was a communist country outside the Soviet bloc. Its wars grew out of conflict among its own peoples, not out of a Soviet decision.
  - hint: Which choices are about a country in the Soviet bloc? What did the Soviet Union do there?

**E9-P03** [Topic 9.1 · LO A · Skill 2.B · medium]  Gorbachev most likely announced the troop cuts described in the last two paragraphs at the United Nations in order to
- (A) warn NATO that Soviet forces would defend the Warsaw Pact countries
- (B) persuade the United States to pull its troops out of West Germany first
- (C) show the West that the Soviet Union wanted to end the arms race
- (D) prepare the way for German reunification under a communist government
KEY: C. Explanation: Gorbachev spoke to the whole world at the UN. By cutting 500,000 troops without asking for anything in return, he tried to show Western leaders that the Soviet Union wanted to end the arms race. The cuts would also save money for the weak Soviet economy.

> Recognizing the passionate yearning for freedom in all dependent peoples and the decisive role of such peoples in the attainment of their independence,

**E9-P04** [Topic 9.9 · LO I · Skill 5.A · Comparison · easy]  The idea that "all peoples have the right to self-determination" is most similar to the ideas of which leader at the Paris Peace Conference of 1919?

- (B) David Lloyd George
- (C) Woodrow Wilson
- (D) Vittorio Orlando
KEY: C. Explanation: In 1918 U.S. President Woodrow Wilson called for self-determination, the right of peoples to choose their own governments. His Fourteen Points and other speeches raised hopes in colonies. In 1919 the idea was applied mostly in Europe, for new states such as Poland and Czechoslovakia. Many colonies in Asia and Africa had to wait until after 1945.
  - why not A: France's leader, Georges Clemenceau, wanted to punish Germany and protect France. France kept its empire and gained new lands as League of Nations mandates.
  - why not B: Britain's leader, David Lloyd George, worked to keep the British Empire together. Britain also gained new lands as mandates.
  - why not D: Italy's leader, Vittorio Orlando, wanted the lands that the Allies had promised Italy in 1915, even where most people were not Italian.
  - hint: Which leader's peace plan of 1918 raised hopes in colonies around the world?

**E9-P05** [Topic 9.9 · LO I · Skill 4.B · medium]  When the declaration was adopted, its demand that "all armed action or repressive measures" against dependent peoples "shall cease" applied most directly to which conflict then under way?

- (B) the Spanish Civil War
- (C) the Soviet Union's war in Afghanistan
- (D) the Algerian War of Independence

  - why not B: The Spanish Civil War (1936 to 1939) was a fight over who would rule Spain, not a colony's fight against a colonial power. It had ended long before 1960.
  - why not C: The Soviet war in Afghanistan began in 1979, almost twenty years after this declaration.
