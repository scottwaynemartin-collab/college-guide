> This conflict has been viewed too tragically, and taken too seriously by the press; the government seeks no conflict. Could the crisis be avoided with honor, the government would gladly welcome it. … But Prussia must keep her strength intact for the favorable moment, which is too often missed. Prussia's boundaries are not favorable to the development of a strong body politic. Not through fine speeches and majority resolutions will the questions of the hour be decided—that was the mistake of 1848 and 1849—but by Iron and Blood. …

> Words to know: intact = whole, not weakened; body politic = a state and its people; resolutions = formal decisions passed by a vote; ministry = the group of officials who run the government; authoritative = having the power to decide; mechanism = the working parts of a machine

**E7-P01** [Topic 7.3 · LO C · Skill 3.A · easy]  Which of the following best describes Bismarck's main argument in the passage?
- (A) Prussia should adopt the British system, in which the king's ministers answer to a majority in parliament.
- (B) Prussia should unite Germany by winning over the liberal majority in its parliament.
- (C) Military power and the king's authority, not parliamentary votes, would decide Prussia's future.
- (D) Prussia should keep its army small so that it would not alarm Austria and France.
KEY: C. Explanation: Bismarck says the big questions of the day will be decided "by Iron and Blood," meaning war and weapons. He also says the ministers serve the king, not a majority in parliament.
  - why not A: He says the opposite. The ministers serve the king, not a majority in parliament: "We, however, are ministers of his Majesty the king."
  - why not B: He mocks "fine speeches and majority resolutions." In 1862 the liberal majority would not pay for his army plans.
  - why not D: He wanted a bigger army. He says Prussia "must keep her strength intact" for "the favorable moment."
  - hint: Look at what Bismarck says will decide "the questions of the hour," and whom he says the ministers serve.

**E7-P02** [Topic 7.1 · LO A · Skill 4.A · medium]  Bismarck's reference to "the mistake of 1848 and 1849" most likely refers to which of the following?
- (A) The crushing of the June Days uprising in Paris
- (B) Prussia's war against Austria for leadership in Germany
- (C) The Frankfurt Parliament's failed attempt to unite Germany
- (D) The creation of the German Confederation at the Congress of Vienna
KEY: C. Explanation: In 1848 an elected assembly met in Frankfurt to unite Germany. It wrote a constitution and offered a German crown to the king of Prussia, who refused it in 1849. Bismarck saw this as proof that speeches and votes could not unite Germany.
  - why not A: The June Days of 1848 were a French workers' uprising put down by the French government. They had nothing to do with uniting Germany.
  - why not B: Prussia fought Austria in 1866, after these speeches of 1862 and 1863.
  - why not D: The Congress of Vienna created the German Confederation in 1815, not in 1848 or 1849.
  - hint: What happened in the German states in 1848 and 1849 when liberals tried to unite Germany through an elected assembly?

**E7-P03** [Topic 7.3 · LO C · Skill 5.B · Causation · medium]  Which later development most clearly illustrates Bismarck's proposed method for achieving German unification?
- (A) Prussia's wars with Denmark, Austria, and France from 1864 to 1871
- (B) The introduction of universal male suffrage for elections to the Reichstag
- (C) Bismarck's request for parliamentary approval of past military spending in 1866
- (D) The German Empire's federal system, which let member states keep their own rulers
KEY: A. Explanation: Bismarck used three short wars to unite Germany under Prussia: against Denmark (1864), Austria (1866), and France (1870–1871). This was "Iron and Blood" in action.
  - why not B: Bismarck did give men the vote in Reichstag elections, but votes did not unite Germany. The passage says unity will come "Not through fine speeches and majority resolutions" but "by Iron and Blood."
  - why not C: In 1866, after the war with Austria, Bismarck asked parliament to approve the money his government had spent without its consent since 1862. This made peace with the liberals at home. It was not a way of uniting Germany.
  - why not D: The federal system shaped how the new empire was governed. It does not show how the German states were brought together.
  - hint: What does "Iron and Blood" mean? Which choice describes Prussia using it?


### Source P2 (questions E7-P04–E7-P06)
> All these considerations, conscious and unconscious, strengthened my opinion that war could be avoided only at the cost of the honour of Prussia and of the national confidence in it. His Majesty the King thereupon decided not to receive the French ambassador again, and sent to tell him through the aide-de-camp on duty that his Majesty had nothing further to communicate to the ambassador.' …
> After I had read out the concentrated edition to my two guests, Moltke remarked: 'Now it has a different ring; it sounded before like a parley; now it is like a flourish in answer to a challenge.' I went on to explain: 'If in execution of his Majesty's order I at once communicate this text, which contains no alteration in or addition to the telegram, not only to the newspapers, but also by telegraph to all our embassies, it will be known in Paris before midnight, and not only on account of its contents, but also on account of the manner of its distribution, will have the effect of a red rag upon the Gallic bull. Success, however, essentially depends upon the impression which the origination of the war makes upon us and others; it is important that we should be the party attacked …'

> ** General Helmuth von Moltke, chief of the Prussian general staff, and Albrecht von Roon, the Prussian minister of war.
— Otto von Bismarck, former chancellor of Germany, describing his actions on July 13, 1870, in his memoirs, written in the 1890s after he left office and published in 1898 (Text from Bismarck, the Man and the Statesman: Being the Reflections and Reminiscences of Otto, Prince von Bismarck, translated under the supervision of A. > Words to know: authorisation = permission; renunciation = giving up a claim; hereditary Prince of Hohenzollern = Prince Leopold, a relative of the Prussian king, who had been offered the throne of Spain; candidature = being a candidate, here for the Spanish throne; aide-de-camp = an officer who assists a king or general; parley = a discussion between enemies to settle a dispute; Gallic = French; vanquished = defeated; origination = the way something starts

**E7-P04** [Topic 7.3 · LO C · Skill 2.B · medium]  According to his account, Bismarck shortened the telegram most likely in order to
- (A) calm French anger so that the quarrel over the Spanish throne could be settled peacefully
- (B) persuade the Spanish government to choose a Hohenzollern prince as its king
- (C) warn Austria not to join France in a war against Prussia
- (D) provoke France into declaring war so that Prussia would seem to be the side attacked
KEY: D. Explanation: Bismarck says the short text would act like "a red rag upon the Gallic bull." He wanted France to look like the attacker. France declared war on July 19, 1870.
  - why not A: He says the new text would act like "a red rag upon the Gallic bull." He wanted to stir up French anger, not calm it.
  - why not B: The prince had already given up his claim. The edited telegram reports "the renunciation of the hereditary Prince of Hohenzollern."
  - why not C: Bismarck aimed the text at Paris and the newspapers. Austria had made peace with Prussia in 1866 and stayed out of the war of 1870.
  - hint: Find the animal comparison Bismarck uses. What does a red rag do to a bull?

**E7-P05** [Topic 7.3 · LO C · Skill 2.C · hard]  Which of the following is the most important limitation of this passage as evidence of Bismarck's thinking in July 1870?
- (A) He had not witnessed the king's meeting with the French ambassador at Ems.
- (B) He based part of his account on a telegram sent by another Prussian official.
- (C) He described a private discussion with military leaders rather than a public debate.
- (D) He wrote decades later and may have wanted to look like the planner of victory.
KEY: D. Explanation: Bismarck wrote his memoirs in the 1890s, after Emperor William II forced him out of office. A historian should check his story against records from 1870, because he may have shaped it to make himself look clever and in control.

  - why not B: Abeken's telegram was written in 1870, so it helps check Bismarck's story. It does not make the account less reliable.
  - why not C: A private talk with generals can show how leaders really made decisions. The bigger problem is that Bismarck described it many years later.

**E7-P06** [Topic 7.2 · LO B · Skill 5.B · Causation · medium]  The events Bismarck describes contributed most directly to which of the following?

### Source P3 (questions E7-P07–E7-P09)
> … Cavour's aim was to push actively and strenuously on the lines which D'Azeglio* had indicated; to court the good-will of France, that perchance some day the French would pour their armies over the Alps to drive Austria from Lombardy and Venetia. He had convinced himself that Piedmont alone could never hope for victory in an offensive war, and he had little faith in a popular rising. … A finer conscience would have hesitated to ask the help of Louis Napoleon. > Light becomes the sole subject of the picture; the interest of the object upon which it plays is secondary.
