# INDEX: AP European History Unit 7 (19th-Century Perspectives and Political Developments, c. 1815–c. 1914) sources

Every text excerpt was copied from raw text downloaded with curl (Internet Archive djvu text, Project Gutenberg, JSTOR Early Journal Content via archive.org) and saved in sources/raw/ (second witnesses in raw/alt/ or alongside). A throwaway script outside the repo machine-checked each stretch between ellipses, brackets, and footnote marks against the raw files after reducing both to lowercase letters only (line-end hyphens joined). Of 110 stretches, 79 matched exactly; the other 31 were read by hand against the raw text and, where needed, a second scan. Every one of those mismatches is a running head or page number, a margin note or footnote interleaved by the OCR, or an OCR slip (for example "thorn" for "them" in Renan, "tae face" in Chamberlain, the garbled "modification" in Comte); each correction is listed in the source's file. Every edition or translation used was published in 1930 or earlier. Images are public-domain files on Wikimedia Commons, confirmed with the Commons API (requests spaced; HTTP 429 responses were waited out and retried), downloaded to raw/commons/ and viewed before the captions were written. Chart and table values were read from page images (archive.org) and checked by eye; the table's rows were also summed against the source's printed totals.

Notes:
- Fall 2026 AP European History CED Unit 7 has nine topics (7.1–7.9) and Learning Objectives A–K (7.3 has C and D; 7.6 has G and H); see ced_unit7_excerpt.txt. Section I Part A: 55 questions in sets of three to four (the 2026 practice exam also uses sets of 2 and 5), at least one paired-text set.
- Calibration (Nov 2026): the timed exam uses sets of 2, 3, and 4 (X4 has 2 questions, X2 and X7 have 4); about a third of all items use short noun-phrase choices; about a quarter link the source to earlier or later developments; X7 (Tilak) is a colonial, non-European voice; X4 and P7 are multi-row data sets.
- Historians and later writers: P3 (Bolton King, 1899), P14 (Moon, 1926), X8 (Mauclair, 1903).
- No source repeats one used in Euro Units 4 or 5 (ae4, ae5) or in AP World Units 5–7. Avoided for that reason or for overlap with the separate Unit 6 bank: Mazzini's "General Instructions for Young Italy" (aw5), Kipling's "The White Man's Burden," Hobson's Imperialism, Karl Pearson, Livingstone, the Berlin Conference General Act, and Casement's Congo report (all aw6); Bernhardi (aw7); the Communist Manifesto, factory testimony, and 1848 sources (Unit 6). Fichte, Metternich, Wordsworth, and Goya were used in ae5 and are not used here.
- Not used: the Gutenberg Herzl (#25282) follows a 1946 revision, so the 1904 revision of d'Avigdor's 1896 translation was used instead. Robinson & Beard's version of Bismarck's Ems account (no. 258) differs slightly from Butler's translation; the bank quotes Butler (1898) directly. A multi-census (1880–1910) language table for Hungary was sought in Seton-Watson's Racial Problems in Hungary (1908) and German, Slav, and Magyar (1916), but neither gives all four censuses, so P7 uses The Statesman's Year-Book 1913.
- Robinson & Beard, Readings in Modern European History, vol. 2, is dated 1909 (both scans print "Copyright, 1909"; it includes events of December 1908). The raw file names (robinsonbeard1908v2_*) keep the year the builder first assumed; the citations in the bank and in these notes give 1909.
- Ellipses mark omitted wording. Footnotes and glossaries are editorial unless credited. The excerpt checker compares letters only, so punctuation was checked by eye against the raw text and page images.
