CHART IN THE BANK: categories Africa, Polynesia (Pacific islands), Asia, Australia, The Americas; 1876 = 10.8, 56.8, 51.5, 100, 27.5; 1900 = 90.4, 98.9, 56.6, 100, 27.2 (decimal commas converted to points). Every value checked against the page image. The chart note restates Supan's definition (share of each region's land area held as colonies by European colonial powers and the United States; Alaska counted as a U.S. colony). Its first sentence was revised on Nov 9, 2026, after the independent check, to read "Each bar shows the percent of a region's land area held as colonies by European powers or by the United States," because the earlier wording ("land area that belonged to European colonial powers or to the United States") could be read as counting U.S. home territory, which Supan's figures do not (Supan counts only colonies, with Alaska as a U.S. colony; the Americas' share would be far above 27.5 percent if the United States' own territory were included). Supan's column of differences (+79.6 etc.) is not shown.

CONTEXT NOTES:
Supan's next sentences (p. 254) say the period's characteristic feature was the partition of Africa and Polynesia, that France sought in overseas expansion a substitute for its lost position in Europe, and that Germany's entry into colonial politics set off the race. The same figures were later quoted by Lenin (1917); the bank cites Supan directly.
