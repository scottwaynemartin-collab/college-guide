Review verdict

I would revise this bank before publishing it as an exact AP exam match. I reviewed all 58 items—33 practice questions and 25 exam questions—including their options, keys, explanations, whyNot lines, rationales, numerical stimuli, and map coordinates.

The most consequential problems are:

P26 and E21 confuse geographic extent with the scale at which data are aggregated. Their current wording makes competing answers defensible.
Several explanations make claims that the supplied evidence cannot establish.
Both the practice bank and exam contain a conspicuous one-of-each-answer-letter pattern in successive five-question blocks.
Some questions do not require their accompanying stimulus.
Several skill tags and difficulty labels overstate the task students actually perform.

Every existing answer letter can be retained with the revisions below. The Mercator and Gall–Peters coordinates are correct; they do not need rebuilding.

For the current framework, College Board identifies the Fall 2020 AP Human Geography CED as the applicable CED for 2026–27. The later corrections document does not establish a new Unit 1 content framework.

1. P26 and E21 — Separate map extent from data aggregation

P26: retain A. E21: retain B.

These are the most important corrections. A map can cover the world while presenting nationally aggregated data. Likewise, a study covering Southeast Asia can compare national figures within a regional geographic extent.

College Board's released 2025 scoring commentary explicitly rejects identifying a Finland map as national merely because it shows Finland; the municipal divisions shown require a local/subnational answer. That directly undermines the rule taught in P26's present explanation.

Replace P26 completely

Stem:

A world map shades each country using that country's average life expectancy. At which scale are the life-expectancy data aggregated for comparison?

Option Replacement
A National, because each mapped value summarizes one country
B Regional, because neighboring countries may have similar life expectancies
C Global, because the display includes countries throughout the world
D Local, because life expectancy is calculated from records about individuals
E Regional, because every country belongs to a larger world region

Explanation:

The map covers the world, but each value summarizes one country. The data are therefore aggregated at the national scale. The area displayed and the level at which data are grouped are different things.

C: "Global describes the map's extent. The map does not combine all countries into one worldwide value."
D: "Records about individuals were combined into national averages; individual or neighborhood values are not shown."
E: "The map reports country values rather than averages for larger world regions."

This preserves A's position while correcting its content.

5. Entire bank — Remove the five-question answer pattern

This is a substantial test-construction problem.

The exam's successive blocks are:

CADBE | ACBED | BACED | ABCED | BAECD

The first 30 practice questions are:

BDACE | BADCE | DACBE | ACDBE | ACBDE | ABCED

Every block contains exactly one A, B, C, D, and E. Neither sequence contains consecutive repeated keys.

Overall balance is fine. This repeated local structure is the problem: students who recognize it can infer an uncertain answer from the other four.

Exact assembly-rule replacement:

Preserve each item's answer key. Order questions without enforcing one of each answer letter in every five items and without prohibiting consecutive repeated letters. Keep shared-stimulus sets together.
