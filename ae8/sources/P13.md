Caption: A street in Guernica lined with ruined buildings. Most of them have lost their roofs and floors, and only parts of their outer walls still stand. Piles of broken stone and wood fill the street. A leafy tree stands at the left, and a few small figures of people can be seen far down the street.
Alt text: A black-and-white photograph of a street lined with ruined buildings whose roofs and floors are gone, with piles of rubble in the street.
Description: A black-and-white photograph of a town street after heavy destruction. In the center, the shells of several tall buildings of three or four stories rise from heaps of rubble. Their roofs and inside floors are gone, so the empty window openings show the pale sky behind them; some walls stand only as narrow, jagged pillars. Broken stone, bricks, and pieces of wood cover the ground in front of the buildings and spill into the street. On the left, a tree in full leaf stands in front of more damaged walls. On the right, the wooden frame of a staircase or balcony rises at the edge of the picture. Far down the street, a few tiny figures of people stand among the ruins. The air looks hazy, as if from dust or smoke.

CONTEXT NOTES:
Planes of the German Condor Legion and the Italian Aviazione Legionaria bombed Guernica on 26 April 1937, during the Spanish Civil War. Britain and France led the Non-Intervention Agreement of August–September 1936, which Germany, Italy and the Soviet Union signed but ignored.
