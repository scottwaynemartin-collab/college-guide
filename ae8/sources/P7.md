CONTEXT NOTES:
French and Belgian troops occupied the Ruhr in January 1923; the German government printed money to pay striking workers, and by November 1923 a dollar bought about 4.2 trillion marks. The Rentenmark (November 1923) ended the inflation; the Dawes Plan (1924) cut reparation payments and brought American loans.
