Caption: Three women workers in caps and long overalls stand among thousands of artillery shells in a huge factory hall. The two in front reach up to guide large shells that hang in a row from an overhead rail. Rows of shells cover the whole floor and stretch into the distance, where a third woman is at work.
Alt text: A black-and-white photograph of women factory workers in caps and overalls reaching up to guide large artillery shells that hang from an overhead rail, in a vast hall whose floor is covered with rows of shells.
Description: A black-and-white photograph taken inside a very large factory building with steel pillars and a high roof. Thousands of artillery shells, each about knee to waist high, stand upright in tight rows that cover the floor from the front of the picture to the far end of the hall. Near the top of the picture a long row of larger shells hangs from an overhead rail. At the left, a young woman in a cap, a long dark overall belted at the waist, and a white collar stands on the shells' level and reaches up with both hands to steady one of the hanging shells. In the center, a second woman in a cap and dark overall stretches both arms up to guide another shell. Farther back on the right, a third woman in the same kind of work clothes reaches up to the rail. Tiny figures of more workers can be seen at the far end of the hall.

CONTEXT NOTES:
By 1918 nearly a million British women worked in munitions; Chilwell was one of the largest filling factories, and an explosion there on 1 July 1918 killed 134 people. The Representation of the People Act 1918 gave the vote to women over 30 who met a property qualification (about 8.4 million); equal franchise came in 1928.
