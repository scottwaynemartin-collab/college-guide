# INDEX: AP World Unit 9 (Globalization, c. 1900–present) sources

Version 2026-11-08b. Revised after an independent review (check_report.md): the unit was globalized, the Salinas set was replaced, and the share of U.S. presidential speeches was cut to three (Eisenhower P1, Reagan P7.2, Johnson P10).

Copyright rule for this unit: only public-domain or openly licensed material. Every text is one of the following:
- a work of the U.S. government (presidential speeches from the Public Papers via the American Presidency Project, a Secretary of State's speech, FRUS, a USAID administrator's speech, the Congressional Record, FBIS transcripts and translations);
- an official text of another state that its own law places outside copyright (China, Copyright Law Art. 5(1); South Africa, Copyright Act 1978 s. 12(8)(a); Mexico, Ley Federal del Derecho de Autor Art. 14(VIII), for the NAFTA treaty text);
- a United Nations General Assembly resolution, a UN treaty text, or the Universal Declaration of Human Rights;
- a text published before 1931 (Government of India report, 1920; Pankhurst, 1914; Hansard, 1927).

Data are CC BY 4.0 (World Bank WDI; Global Carbon Budget via Our World in Data). Images are public-domain U.S. government photographs on Wikimedia Commons. No copyrighted post-1930 non-government text is used, and no paraphrase is presented as a quotation. The statutes relied on are saved in raw/web/ (sa_copyright_act_1978_s12.html, china_copyright_law_2020_npc_translation_chinaiplawupdate.html, mexico_LFDA_diputados.pdf).

Every text excerpt was copied from a raw download saved in sources/raw/ and machine-checked against it: every stretch between ellipses or brackets had to occur in the raw text once both were reduced to letters and digits. The stretches that did not match (11) were read by hand. Each is an OCR slip, a transcription slip, a page marker, or a transcriber's note (crowd reactions), and each correction is listed in its file. The text cut at each ellipsis is listed in each file, taken from the raw text by script.

Balance of voices (21 text sources):
- 7 are U.S. voices: Gaud, H. R. Clinton, Eisenhower, Reagan, Blumenthal, Johnson, Harkin. Only three are presidential speeches.
- 14 are non-U.S. or multinational voices:
  - South Africa: de Klerk, Mandela 1990, Mandela 1998.
  - British India: the 1920 report.
  - China: the State Council white paper.
  - Britain: Pankhurst and Cunliffe-Lister.
  - The UN General Assembly: the UDHR, the NIEO, the HIV/AIDS declaration, and the Millennium Declaration.
  - Treaties: the UNFCCC and NAFTA.
- China now speaks for itself in P13. It is also seen through U.S. sources in P8 and X7.
