# INDEX: AP World Unit 9 (Globalization, c. 1900–present) sources

Copyright rule for this unit: only public-domain or openly licensed material. Every text is either a work of the U.S. government (presidential speeches and statements from the Public Papers via the American Presidency Project, a Secretary of State's speech, FRUS, a CRS report, the Congressional Record, and FBIS transcripts and translations), a government report published before 1931 (Government of India, 1920), or the Universal Declaration of Human Rights. Data are CC BY 4.0 (World Bank WDI; Global Carbon Budget via Our World in Data). Images are public-domain files on Wikimedia Commons. No copyrighted post-1930 non-government text is used, and no paraphrase is presented as a quotation.

Every text excerpt was copied from a raw download saved in sources/raw/ and machine-checked against it: every stretch between ellipses or brackets had to occur in the raw text once both were reduced to letters and digits. The stretches that did not match were read by hand; each is an OCR slip, a transcription slip, a page marker, or a transcriber's note (crowd reactions), and each correction is listed in its file. The text cut at each ellipsis is listed in each file, taken from the raw text by script. Images were confirmed with the Commons API and downloaded at a standard thumbnail size (Wikimedia answered HTTP 429 to most requests; requests were spaced several minutes apart and retried).

Notes:
- Fall 2026 CED Unit 9 has nine topics (9.1–9.9) and Learning Objectives A–I; see ced_unit9_excerpt.txt.
- Global perspectives: South Africa (de Klerk, Mandela) and Mexico (Salinas) through FBIS; British India through the 1920 government report; the UN through the UDHR; China seen through U.S. government sources (FRUS, CRS, Bush, Clinton).
- Salinas (X6) is the one source whose underlying foreign text I could not separately confirm as free of copyright in its home country. It is used under the project's rule that FBIS translations of foreign speeches are acceptable. If that rule is reconsidered, X6 is the set to replace.
- Gaud (X1): no scan of the 1968 printing was found online; the only full text is a typed transcription with a few obvious typing slips, corrected in brackets and listed in X1.md.
- Not used: Chinese government statements on Tiananmen and Deng Xiaoping's 1992 southern tour talks (the FBIS China Daily Reports for 1989 and 1992 are not on the Internet Archive); the Zapatista communiqués and Subcomandante Marcos's interview (FBIS-LAT-94-001 prints an interview from the Italian newspaper L'Unità, which is copyrighted journalism); the 2005 U.S. statement on the UNESCO cultural diversity convention (only a press round-table transcript could be found); the Harper's magazine article that Sen. Harkin inserted into the Congressional Record.
- Passages left out on purpose: a quotation from an unnamed doctor in Bush's 2003 address (P4); Blumenthal's generalizations about the Chinese "national character" (P8); phrases that the CRS report quotes from outside writers (P13).
