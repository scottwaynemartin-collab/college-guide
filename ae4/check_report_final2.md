### 1. E4-P30: the stem rules out all three distractors
The stem says Baroque works "celebrated royal or Catholic authority." A (royal glory), B (Catholic symbols) and C (church altar paintings) are all royal or Catholic. A student can throw out all three using the stem alone and pick D without knowing any art history. C can also be ruled out from the credit line ("shown in Paris in 1785").

**Replace the stem with:**
> Compared with Baroque paintings of the 1600s, this painting places greater emphasis on

**Replace (C) with:**
> (C) intense emotion, shown through the weeping women at the right

**New "why not C":**
> Baroque artists such as Bernini and Rubens stressed intense emotion and movement even more. David sets the women's grief off to one side of a strictly ordered scene.

Keep A, B, D, the explanation, the other "why not" lines and the hint. The explanation already says the change "did not end … strong emotion in painting," so it supports the new C.
*Optional:* B can still be ruled out because the caption shows no religious symbols. If you want every distractor to need knowledge, replace B with "(B) rich color and dramatic contrasts of light and shadow". Why not: "Baroque painters such as Caravaggio and Rubens used even stronger contrasts of light and dark and richer color. David's color and light are controlled and serve a clear, orderly design."

### 2. E4-P28: each choice's own description lets students match the caption
Every choice defines its style, so students can answer without knowing any style:
- Baroque says "glorify the Catholic Church and kings," but the caption shows no church or king.
- Romanticism says "wild nature," but the setting is a stone hall.
- Rococo says "playful scenes … love and leisure," but the women are weeping.
- The key says "plain settings," and the caption says "In a plain stone hall."

(The last round suggested "simple settings." The bank now uses "plain," which repeats the caption word.) This is a style-identification (1.A) item, so the knowledge it tests is what each style looks like, and the descriptions give that knowledge away.

**Replace the four choices with the style names alone:**
> (A) Baroque
> (B) Neoclassicism
> (C) Romanticism
> (D) Rococo

**New "why not A":**
> Baroque art of the 1600s used swirling motion, rich color, and dramatic light, often to glorify the Church and kings. David's figures stand still in a plain, orderly space.

**New "why not C":**
> Romantic painters came later, in the early 1800s, and stressed strong feeling, wild nature, and the individual. David shows a planned, orderly scene of duty.

Keep the "why not D" line, the explanation and the hint.
