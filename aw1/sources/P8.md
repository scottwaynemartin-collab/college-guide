Tribute Owed to the Rulers of Tenochtitlan by Two Provinces of the Mexica (Aztec) Empire (selected items)

Item | Quauhnahuac province: 16 towns near the Valley of Mexico | Xoconochco province: 8 towns on the Pacific coast, far to the south

Large woven cloaks | 1,200 loads, twice a year | —

Paper | 8,000 sheets, twice a year | —

Cups | 2,000, twice a year | 800 cups for drinking cacao, every six months

Warrior costumes and shields decorated with feathers | 8 of each, once a year | —

Maize, beans, chia, and amaranth | 4 large wooden bins, once a year | —

Cacao beans | — | 200 loads, every six months

Feathers | — | 800 handfuls of yellow feathers and 800 handfuls of green quetzal feathers, every six months

Jaguar skins | — | 40, every six months

Jade stones | — | 2 strings, every six months

Note shown under the table: The codex was painted by Indigenous artists, with notes in Spanish, in Mexico City about 1541, twenty years after the Spanish conquest. It was probably ordered by the first Spanish viceroy (governor) to send to the king of Spain. Historians believe its tribute pages were based on older Mexica tribute records. The notes say the Mexica rulers placed tax collectors and a governor from Tenochtitlan in the towns of Quauhnahuac "that they should not rebell."

Source: Codex Mendoza, about 1541, tribute section; English translation of the Spanish notes published by Samuel Purchas in Purchas His Pilgrimes (1625; reprinted 1906, Vol. XV)

ADAPTATION / OCR NOTES:
- Table compiled from Purchas's lists; every number checked against the text: Quauhnahuac - 1,200 burthens of great mantels of twisted yarne (twice a year); 8,000 sheets of paper per payment ("Eight Reames... twice a yeere; so that in all it was every yeere sixteene thousand sheets"); 2,000 Xicharas (cups) twice a year; 8 pieces of armour and as many targets (shields) with feathers, once a year; 4 great troxes (bins) of maize, frisoles (beans), chian and huautli (amaranth), once a year. Xoconochco - 200 burthens of cacao; 40 tigres (jaguar) skins; 800 handfuls of yellow feathers; 800 handfuls of green quetzal feathers; 2 great strings of chalchihuitl (jade) stones; 800 tecomates (cups) for cacao; "Al which they tributed every sixe moneths."
- Only selected items are shown (the table title says "Selected items"). Quoted phrase "that they should not rebell" is verbatim from p. 443.
- Note in the table: the codex was commissioned c. 1541, probably by Viceroy Antonio de Mendoza, for Charles V; Purchas's marginal date "c. 1525" is not used.
