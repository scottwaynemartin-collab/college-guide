Title shown: Estimated Rise in Pay Rates for Threshing Grain in Five Regions of England, 1350–1400, Compared with Rates Before 1348
Note shown: Threshing is beating harvested grain to separate the seeds from the stalks. The economic historian who made these estimates studied records of the wages paid on English farms and estates. Each figure combines the pay for threshing wheat, barley, and oats, and shows how much higher the average pay from 1350 to 1400 was than the pay before 1348. The figures are rates paid for the work, not yearly earnings, and they are not adjusted for changes in prices.
Source line shown: James E. Thorold Rogers, Six Centuries of Work and Wages: The History of English Labour (London, 1884), p. 233
Values: Eastern Counties 60, Midland Counties 73, Southern Counties 48, Western Counties 37, Northern Counties 59

NOTES:
- Rogers's sentence: "If we take the whole period from 1350 to 1400 … the final rise in the prices of threshing, taking the three principal kinds of grain together, is 60 per cent. in the Eastern Counties, 73 in the Midland, 48 in the Southern, 37 in the Western, and 59 in the Northern."
