Title shown: The Route from Tana to Cambalec (Beijing), Stage by Stage, as Listed in a Florentine Merchant's Handbook, about 1340
Note shown: Tana was an Italian trading post at the mouth of the Don River, on the Sea of Azov (an arm of the Black Sea). Cambalec was the capital of Cathay (northern China). The author, Francesco Balducci Pegolotti, was an agent of a Florentine banking company; he gathered this information for other merchants. He adds that at Organci "there is a ready sale for goods" and that at Cassai the merchant can sell his silver for paper money, "for that is a most active place of business."
Source line shown: Francesco Balducci Pegolotti, The Practice of Commerce, about 1340, translated by Henry Yule, revised by Henri Cordier, Cathay and the Way Thither, vol. III (London: Hakluyt Society, 1914), pp. 146–149. Modern names added in parentheses.
Rows:
- Tana to Gittarchan (Astrakhan) | 25 days (10–12 days by horse-wagon) | Ox-wagon or horse-wagon
- Gittarchan to Sara (Sarai) | 1 day | By river
- Sara to Saracanco (Saraichik) | 8 days | By river (or by land)
- Saracanco to Organci (Urgench) | 20 days | Camel-wagon
- Organci to Oltrarre (Otrar) | 35–40 days | Camel-wagons
- Oltrarre to Armalec (Almaliq) | 45 days | Pack-asses
- Armalec to Camexu (Zhangye, in Gansu) | 70 days | Asses
- Camexu to a river (name left blank in the manuscript) | 45 days | On horseback
- Down the river to Cassai (Hangzhou) | Not given | By river
- Cassai to Cambalec (Beijing) | 30 days | Not given

NOTES:
- Table built from ch. I: each stage, time, and carrier as Pegolotti gives them; "Not given" where he gives none. The river after Camexu is left blank in the manuscript (Yule's note).
- Modern names follow Yule's notes: Gittarchan = Astrakhan, Sara = Sarai, Saracanco = Saraichik, Organci = Urgench, Oltrarre = Otrar, Armalec = Almaliq, Camexu = Ganzhou (Kan-chau), today's Zhangye in Gansu (the table says "Zhangye, in Gansu" so it is not confused with the modern city of Ganzhou in Jiangxi), Cassai = Hangzhou, Cambalec = Beijing.
- Quoted in the note: "in that city there is a ready sale for goods" (Organci) and "for that is a most active place of business" (Cassai).
