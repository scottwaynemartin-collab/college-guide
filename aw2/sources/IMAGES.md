## X4: Abraham Cresques (attributed), Jewish mapmaker on the island of Majorca (in today's Spain), detail from the Catalan Atlas, a map of the known world drawn on parchment, 1375

- Questions: U2-E11–U2-E13; topic(s): 2.4 (LO H), 2.4 (LO I), 2.5 (LO J)

- Thumbnail used (src): https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/ea/Catalan_Atlas_BNF_Sheet_6_Mansa_Musa.jpg/960px-Catalan_Atlas_BNF_Sheet_6_Mansa_Musa.jpg (960 x 685)

- Commons metadata: "Sheet 6 out of 12. Detail showing Mansa Musa sitting on a throne and holding a gold coin"; Artist "attributed to Abraham Cresques"; DateTimeOriginal 1375 AD; Credit: Gallica Digital Library, digital ID btv1b55002481n; LicenseShortName "Public domain"; Copyrighted False
- Holder: Bibliothèque nationale de France, Paris (Catalan Atlas, Espagnol 30)
- Checked: Viewed after download. Labels read in the image: tagaza, tenbuch, ciutat de melli, geugeu, buda, sudam(?), and GINYIA; the Catalan note at the upper right begins "aquest senyor negre es a[pellat] musse melly senyor dels negres de gineua". No camel rider is in this crop, so no question or caption mentions one. The caption names only labels that can be read in the image; it does not translate the note's praise of the king's gold (which would give away E11).
- Caption shown to students: This section of a hand-drawn map shows lands south of the Sahara. A king sits on a throne in the center. He wears a gold crown, holds up a round gold object in one hand, and holds a scepter in the other. Small drawings of towns surround him. Labels in Catalan name several of them, including Tagaza (Taghaza), Tenbuch (Timbuktu), Ciutat de Melli (the city of Mali), and Geugeu (Gao). A note in Catalan at the upper right names the king as Musse Melly.

The note above the caravan reads "aquesta caravana es partida del imperi de sarra per anar al catayo" (this caravan has set out from the empire of Sarai to go to Cathay); the caption reports this in plain words without quotation marks. Glossary glosses Sarra only as a city on the lower Volga, so it does not give away P44.
- Caption shown to students: This section of a hand-drawn map shows a caravan crossing rocky ground. Camels loaded with packs walk in front, with men on foot walking behind and beside them. Behind them ride men on horses, wearing tall pointed hats in many colors. Below the caravan are small drawings of walled towns with flags. A note in Catalan above the caravan says that it set out from the empire of Sarra to go to Catayo.
