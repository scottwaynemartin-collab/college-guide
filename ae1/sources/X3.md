IMAGE NOTES:
The caption and description were written from the 1280 px thumbnail. The instruments on the upper shelf are described in general terms (globe of the heavens, sundials, a quadrant, 'another instrument for measuring the positions of the stars'); the lower shelf holds a terrestrial globe, a merchants' arithmetic book with a ruler in it, a lute with a broken string, a case of flutes, dividers, and an open hymnbook. The National Gallery identifies the books as Peter Apian's arithmetic for merchants (1527) and Johann Walter's Lutheran hymnbook (1524); the caption does not name them or interpret their symbolism. The anamorphic skull and the half-hidden crucifix are described; the crucifix appears only in the long description.

CONTEXT NOTES:
Dinteville was the French ambassador in London in 1533, the year Henry VIII married Anne Boleyn and broke with Rome; de Selve was bishop of Lavaur. E1-E08 to E1-E10 use the painting for Northern Renaissance naturalism, the science of navigation, and printed books.
