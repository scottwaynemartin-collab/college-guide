# INDEX: AP European History Unit 1 (Renaissance and Exploration, c. 1450–c. 1648) sources

Every text excerpt was copied from raw text downloaded with curl (Internet Archive djvu text and Project Gutenberg) and saved in sources/raw/; where a second witness was used it sits alongside (Gutenberg #5000 for Richter's Leonardo, #2130 for Burnet's Utopia, #2272 for Raleigh, #3600 for Cotton–Hazlitt's Montaigne, and Payne's 1880 first edition). A throwaway script outside the repo machine-checked each stretch between ellipses, brackets, and footnote marks against the raw files after reducing both to lowercase letters only (line-end hyphens joined). Of 122 stretches, 97 matched exactly; the other 25 were read by hand against the raw text and, where the OCR was poor, against page images saved in raw/img/ (Bacon p. 456, Helps pp. 379–382, Donnan pp. 42 and 123–124, Pacioli p. 33). Every one of those mismatches is a running head, a margin note or footnote interleaved by the OCR, a digit read for a letter ('0 thou', 'did 1 read'), another OCR slip ('Xature', 'Grod', 'they bob' for 'they are', 'Dig' for 'big'), or the tilde in 'Doña', which the OCR drops; each is listed in the source's file. Every edition or translation used was published in 1930 or earlier. Images are public-domain files on Wikimedia Commons, confirmed with one batched Commons API request (after an HTTP 429 on an earlier request, requests were spaced and retried), downloaded to raw/commons/ and viewed before the captions were written. The table's values (X4) are Rogers's own figures in words, read in the text and checked on the page images.

Notes:
- Fall 2026 AP European History CED Unit 1 has eleven topics (1.1–1.11) and Learning Objectives A–O; see ced_unit1_excerpt.txt. The bank's exam has 25 questions in 8 sets of 2–4 (per the calibration note, sets of 2–5 are allowed); practice has 16 sets of 3.
- Overlap avoided: no source repeats one used in ae4 or ae5 or in the other Euro units' stimuli as of this build. ae4 uses Bacon's Novum Organum (a different work from X6's History of Henry VII); ae2 uses Montaigne's 'Of Liberty of Conscience' (Essays II.19), a different essay from P12.2's 'Of Cannibals' (I.30); ae6 cites Thorold Rogers only as editor of Cobden's speeches. Robinson's Readings in European History, vol. 2 (P4), is also used by ae5, but for different documents (the Berlin Decree and others). Not reused from AP World Unit 4 (aw4): the Columbus–Santángel letter, Las Casas, Zurara, Cortés, Barbosa, Acosta, Mun, and the Cantino planisphere.
- Two attribution errors were caught while writing these files and corrected in stimuli.py: the Raleigh passage (P11) is from the address 'To the Reader', not the dedication to Howard and Cecil; the Hawkins account (P14) is Hakluyt's own note ('[Note by Hakluyt.]' in Payne), not a text 'written from information Hawkins gave'. Page ranges were also tightened against the running heads (P1 pp. 275–277, P5 pp. 57–58, P9.1 p. 80, P13 pp. 465–466, 470, 494–495), and P15's credit no longer calls 'big' a corrected misprint (the page image shows 'big'; 'Dig' is an OCR slip).
- Not used: Robinson's Readings vol. 1 (1904) and Burckhardt vol. 2 were downloaded while searching and are kept in raw/ but not used; H. F. Brown, The Venetian Printing Press (1891), T. L. De Vinne, The Invention of Printing (1876) and W. J. B. Crotch, The Prologues and Epilogues of William Caxton (1928) were downloaded as possible witnesses for the printing set but not needed (Knight's modernized text was used and checked against itself; Crotch's original-spelling text would be too hard for students).
- Glossaries ('Words to know'), footnotes marked '*', image captions and descriptions, and the X4 table layout are editorial.
- Review fixes (version 2026-11-09b): X8.1's gloss on 'Bresa' corrected (Bresse was part of the Duchy of Savoy, not Charles's land; see X8.1.md); P12.1's attribution now places the first island 'in the Bahamas', and its credit says the journal 'survives only in a shortened copy made by Bartolomé de Las Casas' (see P12.1.md).
