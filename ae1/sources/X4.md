DATA (where each value comes from):

- Basket: 'three quarters of wheat, three of malt, and two of oatmeal' for a family 'for a twelvemonth' (p. 389, raw lines 16722–16723).
- 1495: farm laborer 'fifteen weeks of ordinary work'; artisan 'ten weeks' (p. 389, raw lines 16723–16725). Wages actually paid.
- 1564: laborer 'more than forty weeks'; artisan 'thirty-two weeks' work' (p. 390, raw lines 16777–16782). Wages from the Rutlandshire magistrates' schedule of June 1564; Rogers notes wheat 'at a scarcity price' that year (raw line 16775), the basis of the note 'Wheat prices were unusually high in 1564.'
- 1593: laborer 'The work of a whole year would not supply the labourer' (shown as 'more than a full year of work'); artisan 'forty weeks' labour' (p. 391, raw lines 16796–16799). Wages fixed by the East Riding of York magistrates, April 26, 1593.
- 1610: artisan 'forty-three weeks'; laborer's wages 'would be insufficient, even if he worked for fifty-two weeks in the year' (pp. 391–392, raw lines 16833–16846). Wages fixed by the Rutland magistrates at Oakham, April 28, 1610.

ADAPTATION IN THE BANK (every cut):
Table title, column labels, the explanatory note and the source line are editorial. Rows chosen (since version 2026-11-09c): 1495, 1564, 1593. The 1610 row (artisan 'forty-three weeks'; laborer short 'even if he worked for fifty-two weeks') was dropped because Rogers's stated figure does not match his own prices and wage: wheat 40s. 4d., malt 15s. 4½d., and oatmeal 'about 43s. 4d. a quarter' (p. 391, read on the page image) make the basket 3 × 484d. + 3 × 184½d. + 2 × 520d. = 3,045½d.; at the '9d. a day' he names, a six-day week earns 54d., so the basket takes about 56 weeks, not 43 (43 weeks would fit about 1s. a day, the master artisans' summer rate). The title now ends '1495–1593 (Rogers's Estimates)', and the note says the wages were 'actually paid in 1495' and set by officials in Rutland (1564) and the East Riding (1593), and that the figures are 'estimates for selected places, not averages for all of England'. E1-E11's stem now reads '1495 to 1593' and its explanation cites 40 weeks in 1593. Omitted: 1533 (artisan 14–15 weeks, but no clear laborer figure: 'nearly double' in wheat and oats), 1597 (no weeks figure; Chester schedule mutilated) and 1651 (outside the unit).

CONTEXT NOTES:
The 1564 assessment followed the Statute of Artificers (1563), which Rogers calls 'the Act to which I have several times referred, and which had just been passed'. The table is used for the price revolution, 1495–1593 (E1-E11) and its effect on poverty and the poor laws of 1598 and 1601 (E1-E12).
