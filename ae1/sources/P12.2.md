ADAPTATION IN THE BANK (every cut):
Paragraph 1 opens mid-sentence after 'Now, to return to my subject,' and is cut after 'usage of all things.' (the paragraph continues 'They are savages at the same rate that we say fruits are wild ...'). Then about 2,270 words are cut (the description of the Tupinambá, their warfare, and their killing and eating of prisoners, and the Portuguese way of killing prisoners that they began to copy). Paragraph 2 begins mid-sentence at 'I am not sorry' and is complete to '... after he is dead.', the end of the paragraph. The footnote is editorial. Since version 2026-11-09c it also reports what comes just before 'I am not sorry': the Tupinambá, having seen the Portuguese, 'who were in league with their enemies', kill prisoners by setting them in the earth to the waist, shooting them with arrows and then hanging them, thought this way 'must needs be more painful than theirs' and 'began to leave their old way, and to follow this' (raw lines 13493–13510 of the 1877 scan, p. 259).

CONTEXT NOTES:
Montaigne's 'neighbours and fellow-citizens' killed 'under colour of piety and religion' points to the French Wars of Religion (1562–1598), including the St. Bartholomew's Day Massacre of 1572 (E1-P36).
