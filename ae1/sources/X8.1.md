CONTEXT NOTES:
Who Gorrevod was (glossary checked in the 2026-11-09b review fix): Laurent (Lorenzo) de Gorrevod (c. 1470–1529) was a nobleman from Bresse who became governor of Bresse in 1504, when Bresse was Savoyard territory; it stayed with the House of Savoy until the Treaty of Lyon (1601) gave it to France. He served Charles's aunt Margaret of Austria (widow of Duke Philibert II of Savoy, later governor of the Netherlands), sat on her council, and became Charles's chamberlain (1515) and a member of his council, as the license says ('member of my Council'). Bresse was never Charles's land; the bank's gloss now reads 'Bresse, a region then part of the Duchy of Savoy, now in eastern France' (it earlier said, wrongly, 'a region in Charles's lands'). Margaret seems to have held lands in Savoy as her dower after 1504, so the gloss names the duchy rather than saying the duke himself ruled Bresse. Checked against Wikipedia, 'Laurent de Gorrevod' (Bresse 'then ruled by the House of Savoy'; governor 1504; confidant and envoy of Margaret of Austria; member of her council; Charles's 2nd chamberlain 1515; the 1518 license) and 'Archduchess Margaret of Austria' (Bresse 'then Savoyard territory'), read Nov. 8, 2026.

This is the first large asiento-style license for carrying Africans directly from Africa to the Americas; Gorrevod sold it to Genoese merchants. Paired with Brandão (X8.2) to show the change from a limited royal license (4,000) to a regular trade of 'ten or twelve thousand' a year from one port (E1-E25).
