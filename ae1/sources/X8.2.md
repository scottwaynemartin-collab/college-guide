OCR / TEXT NOTES:
The OCR of the first eleven lines of the letter on p. 123 is badly garbled ('negroes ^ ™ ^ Y°U W°uld like t0 know', 'Itult'wkh i, w'“ virtuous'); the whole first paragraph was therefore read from the page image raw/img/donnan1930_p123_leaf140.jpg, which shows the text exactly as in the bank, including 'São Thomé', 'Loando—all learned and virtuous men—find fault with it' and 'illicit. There-fore'. On p. 124: 'dlega y' and 'lllega >' are 'illegally'; 'hecause' is 'because'; 'here-out of whom many are saved-' is printed with dashes ('here—out of whom many are saved—because'); 'num¬ber', 'impos¬sibility' joined.

ADAPTATION IN THE BANK (every cut):
Paragraph 1 is complete to 'without any scruple.'; then cut of about 275 words (the argument that buyers in America can buy in good faith, the Jesuits' own purchases 'from other negroes', and the warning that captives will always claim they were taken illegally 'in the hope that they will be given their liberty.'). Paragraph 2 begins mid-paragraph at 'I declare, moreover,' and runs to '... many and legally captured.', the end of the passage Donnan prints. Donnan's date line '[March 12, 1610.]' is given in the attribution.

CONTEXT NOTES:
Donnan's note: Sandoval, 'considerably in advance of his age, was greatly troubled by scruples regarding the legality of the slave trade' and had written to Brandão. Sandoval later published De instauranda Aethiopum salute (Seville, 1627).
