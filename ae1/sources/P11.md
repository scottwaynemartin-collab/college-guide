ADAPTATION IN THE BANK (every cut):
Paragraph 1 opens mid-paragraph at 'But if we now consider'. Cut after 'with their artillery' of ', and that no year is less infortunate, but that many vessels, treasures, and people are devoured, and yet notwithstanding he beginneth again like a storm to threaten shipwrack to us all;' (about 35 words). The paragraph is complete from 'we shall find' to 'monarchies of Europe.'; it continues 'If the Spanish king can keep us from foreign enterprises ...'. Paragraph 2 is cut from the middle of a long sentence about 390 words later (it begins after '... the time which our enemies embrace cannot be had again to advantage,') and ends after 'which he possesseth' (the sentence continues '; which if the same be considered and followed, ...').

CONTEXT NOTES:
'Eighty-eight' is the Armada year, 1588. England and Spain were at war from 1585 to 1604. An earlier version of the bank's attribution called the passage a letter to two ministers; it was corrected to 'To the Reader' after checking the headings in both witnesses.
