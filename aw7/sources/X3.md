What the image shows (checked by viewing): "Helft uns siegen!" in large black Fraktur letters at top; framed picture of a German soldier from the chest up in a gray steel helmet (Stahlhelm) with chin strap and gray tunic; shadowed, tired face with eyes looking straight out; a round gas mask hanging on his chest; his hand gripping a wooden post wrapped in barbed wire, with more barbed wire at left; signature "Erler" at lower left of the picture; "zeichnet die Kriegsanleihe" in large letters at the bottom.
Caption choices: the caption translates the poster's words (students cannot be expected to read German Fraktur) and describes only what is visible. Two cylindrical objects at the soldier's lower right may be stick grenades but are not clear at this size, so they are not mentioned. The caption does not say what the war loan paid for; the glossary defines "war loan" only.
Context: Germany raised nine war loans (Kriegsanleihen) from 1914 to 1918; steel helmets entered German service in 1916; British blockade and the "turnip winter" of 1916–1917; Kiel mutiny and revolution, November 1918; Wilhelm II's abdication announced 9 November 1918.
