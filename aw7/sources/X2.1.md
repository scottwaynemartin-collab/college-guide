CUTS (in order):
1. Para 1 = the second paragraph of section I ("Yet in a very real sense ... wars to-morrow."). Everything after it in sections I–III is cut (…), down to "Thus, more and more, the Imperialists have concentrated on Africa." (also cut).
2. Para 2 begins at "The greater the concentration the more deadly the rivalry." and runs to "at each other's throats to-day." (complete).
3. Para 3 = "The present world war is, then, ... particularly in Africa." Cut after it (…): "'We want no inch of French territory,' said Germany to England ..." and the rest of section III.
4. Para 4 = "We, then, who want peace, must remove the real causes of war." Cut (…): "We have extended gradually our conception of democracy ... If we want real peace and lasting culture, however, we must go further." Then "We must extend the democratic ideal to the yellow, brown, and black peoples." (end of excerpt).
Racist epithets quoted by Du Bois elsewhere in the article (section II) are not in the excerpt.

FOOTNOTE: Fashoda (1898) and Agadir (1911) identified; notes that "Dark Continent," "darker world," and the color terms are Du Bois's period wording.
CONTEXT: Du Bois edited The Crisis (NAACP) from 1910; he organized the Pan-African Congress in Paris, February 1919. Entente Cordiale (8 April 1904) settled Anglo-French disputes over Egypt and Morocco.
