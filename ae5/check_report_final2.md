## SHOULD-FIX

### A. Distractors still open to test-taking tricks

1. **E5-P02 B: "any" is an absolute on a wrong answer.** The word "any" lets test-wise students drop B without knowledge. C is also the only hedged choice and, until now, the longest one.
   - B → `Mahan wrote more than a century after the war, when few written records from the 1750s and 1760s still survived.`
   - why not B → `Many records of the war survived. Historians in 1890 could use printed letters, reports, and earlier histories, and Mahan even quotes Henri Martin's history.`

2. **E5-E17 A: "exactly" is an absolute on a wrong answer.**
   - A → `Europe's rulers should undo the changes made since 1789 and bring back the old regime`
   - A rationale → `Incorrect. Metternich tells governments to keep their institutions "both ancient and modern" and warns that it is "dangerous to touch them." He does not ask them to undo the changes made since 1789.` (Both quotes are in the excerpt.)

3. **E5-E14: only the key names Napoleon.** D is the only choice that names Napoleon or the emperor, which the whole catechism is about, and it is also the longest. This is the same word-match problem that was fixed in E15.
   - A → `prepare French Catholics to elect their own priests and bishops under Napoleon's new church laws`
   - B → `persuade Protestants and Jews in Napoleon's empire to convert to the Catholic faith`
   - C → `teach French children that Emperor Napoleon would keep the Church out of the state's affairs`
   - D: no change.
   - The existing A, B and C rationales still work.

4. **E5-E15 B: "new" gives B away.** The phrase "a new civic religion" can be ruled out by its wording alone, since the catechism speaks of "the holy religion of our fathers."
   - B → `the Cult of the Supreme Being of 1794`
   - B rationale → `Incorrect. Robespierre's Cult of the Supreme Being was a new civic religion, not the Catholic faith, and it came before Napoleon took power.` (This also removes the non-excerpt quote marks; see item 9.)
