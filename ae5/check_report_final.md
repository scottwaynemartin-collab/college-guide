**E5-P29.** Choices A, B and C would all make the blockade work better, so none of them can explain why it failed. Students can reach D by logic alone.
- A → `Prussia and Austria refused to join the system and kept their ports open to British ships`
  - why not A: `Prussia was forced to close its ports to British goods by the Treaty of Tilsit in 1807, and Austria was also pressed into the system.`
- B → `Britain made peace with France in 1807 and won back the right to trade in European ports`
  - why not B: `Britain stayed at war with Napoleon from 1803 until 1814. It never made peace to reopen European trade.`
- C → `Napoleon canceled the decree in 1808 after French merchants complained that it hurt their own trade`
  - why not C: `Napoleon tightened the system with new decrees in 1807 and 1810 and kept it until his empire fell. He sold some trading licenses but never canceled the decree.`

**E5-E06.** Choices A, B and D are successes or continuities, not dangers. Students can rule them out from the stem alone.
- A → `the French army's defeat at Valmy, which let the Prussian army march on to Paris`
  - A: `Incorrect. The French won at Valmy in September 1792, and the Prussians retreated. Paris was not taken until 1814.`
- B → `a civil war that broke out in 1790 after Louis XVI refused to accept the Declaration of the Rights of Man`
  - B: `Incorrect. Louis XVI accepted the Declaration in October 1789, and no civil war broke out in 1790. The great revolts, such as the Vendée, began in 1793.`
- D → `the National Assembly's seizure of all private land, which it divided equally among the peasants`
  - D: `Incorrect. The Declaration of 1789 protected property as a sacred right. The Assembly took and sold the Church's lands, but it did not seize all private land or divide it equally.`
