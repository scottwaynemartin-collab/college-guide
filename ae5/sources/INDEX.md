# INDEX: AP European History Unit 5 (Conflict, Crisis, and Reaction in the Late 18th Century, c. 1648–1815) sources

Every text excerpt was copied from raw text downloaded with curl and saved in sources/raw/ (second scans in raw/alt/ or alongside), then machine-checked stretch by stretch against the raw text with a throwaway script: each stretch between ellipses, brackets, or footnote marks had to occur in the raw text after case, punctuation, whitespace, and line-end hyphens were normalized. Stretches that did not match exactly were read by hand; every mismatch is an OCR slip, a running head or page break, or an interleaved marginal note (Robinson and Robinson & Beard print side notes), and each is listed in its file. Every edition or translation used was published in 1930 or earlier. Images are public-domain files on Wikimedia Commons, confirmed with the Commons API (spaced requests after HTTP 429 responses) and downloaded to raw/commons/ to write captions. Chart values were read from the page image (archive.org IIIF) and every running total was checked against the printed additions and repayments.

Notes:
- Fall 2026 AP European History CED Unit 5 has nine topics (5.1–5.9) and Learning Objectives A–J (5.6 has F and G); see ced_unit5_excerpt.txt. The CED's exam section says the 55 MC questions come in sets of three to four with at least one paired-text set.
- Not used because no verified pre-1930 English text was found: Sieyès, What Is the Third Estate? (only a partial 1790s English extract is recorded; the 1791 English 'Essay on Privileges' exists but the ECCO OCR is poor), Olympe de Gouges's Declaration of the Rights of Woman. Robespierre's February 1794 speech exists in an English translation (Philadelphia, 1794; archive.org ECCO scan), but the scan's gutter cuts off the first letters of each line, so the Terror is represented by Desmoulins (Robinson & Beard, 1908).
- Not used to avoid overlap with ae4 (whose raw folder holds them): Condorcet's essay on women's citizenship (Gutenberg #31550), Rousseau's Emile (Gutenberg #5427), and Arthur Young's July 1789 'poor woman' passage. Women's participation is represented by Michelet (1855 translation) paired with the Napoleonic Code; Rousseau's role in Romanticism is tested through outside knowledge (E22) rather than a Rousseau passage.
- Not reused from aw5 (World Unit 5): the Declaration of the Rights of Man, Dessalines's 1804 declaration, Wollstonecraft's Vindication, Rousseau's Social Contract.
- Greer, The Incidence of the Terror (1935), was considered for a chart, but the archive.org copy is lending-only and its tables could not be checked, so it was not used.
