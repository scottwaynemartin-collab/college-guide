License basis (two layers): (1) the underlying Russian letter is an official document of a state body: a formal diplomatic communication from the head of the Soviet government (Chairman of the Council of Ministers) to the head of another state, issued in that capacity and broadcast by Moscow radio. Russian Civil Code art. 1259(6)(1) excludes from copyright "official documents of state bodies … (laws, other normative acts, judicial decisions, other materials of a legislative, administrative and judicial nature), official documents of international organizations, and their official translations"; the 1993 Russian copyright law (art. 8) already excluded official documents and their official translations, so the letter was not protected in Russia on the URAA date and U.S. copyright should not have been restored under 17 U.S.C. § 104A. (2) The English text is the State Department's own translation, a U.S. government work (17 U.S.C. § 105), printed in FRUS. Printing in a U.S. government publication is not by itself the basis. Judged adequate. If a reviewer considers a head of government's letter outside art. 1259(6), the source should be replaced (for example by an official Soviet government statement on Cuba).
