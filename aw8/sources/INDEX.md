# INDEX: AP World Unit 8 (Cold War and Decolonization, c. 1900–present) sources

Revision of Nov 8, 2026 (bank version 2026-11-08b).

Copyright rule for this unit: there is no blanket claim. Each source has its own license basis, given in the table and argued in its own file. The bases used are:
- (a) a work of the U.S. government (17 U.S.C. § 105);
- (b) published in 1930 or earlier;
- (c) an official document of a foreign state that the state's own law excludes from copyright, in an official translation or an English text issued by that state (PRC Copyright Law art. 5(1); Vietnam Law on Intellectual Property art. 15(2); Russian Civil Code art. 1259(6)). Because such texts were not protected in their source countries, U.S. copyright was not restored under 17 U.S.C. § 104A;
- (d) an official record or document of the United Nations or another intergovernmental body (UN: ST/AI/189/Add.9/Rev.2, Commons template PD-UN-doc);
- (e) an open license (UK legislation under the Open Government Licence v3.0).

Printing in a U.S. government publication is never used as a basis by itself. No copyrighted post-1930 non-government text is quoted, and nothing is paraphrased as a quotation.

Every text excerpt was copied from raw text saved in sources/raw/ and machine-checked against it: every stretch between ellipses, brackets, or footnote marks had to occur in the raw text once both were reduced to letters and digits. Where the raw OCR is broken, the passages used were read by eye from the page images and transcribed in a separate file (raw/un_a43pv72_transcribed.txt, raw/un_sres82_83_1950_en.txt, raw/prc_ocr_corrections.txt). Seven stretches still differ from the raw text; each is a known case read by hand: an OCR slip (X5, two stretches; P1's decorated capital), a page header inside a sentence (P3, P8), a typo in the Avalon transcription corrected from the Public Papers (X8 "it poses"), and a FRUS editors' bracketed correction (P12.1 "Amritsar").

Replaced in the Nov 8 revision (with the reason):
- X6 Mao Zedong, speech of 2 Feb 1959 (JPRS translation): Mao's text is under restored U.S. copyright (Mao d. 1976). Replaced by the 1954 PRC Constitution.
- X7 U.S. memo of Nkrumah's talk with Nixon: a U.S. record of an African voice. Replaced by the Bandung Final Communiqué.
- P4 NSC-68: replaced by the Warsaw Pact, so the Soviet bloc's own alliance text is read.
- P5 U.S. embassy summary of Ren Bishi and Mao speeches (1948): replaced by the PRC's Agrarian Reform Law, the Chinese government's own text.
- P9 Nixon's report on Africa: replaced by the Ghana Independence Act and UN Resolution 1514 (more Topic 8.5 items).
- P10 Source 1, Truman on Korea: replaced by UN Security Council Resolutions 82–83.
- P13: UN Security Council Resolution 1267 added as Source 2.
- P16 U.S. memo of Gorbachev's lunch with Reagan and Bush: replaced by Gorbachev's own UN speech of the same day.

Considered and not used:
- Sukarno's Bandung opening speech (1955): drafted, then dropped. Indonesia's copyright law in force in 1996 protected speeches (art. 11) and allowed only a use exception for government releases (art. 13(b)), so U.S. restoration cannot be ruled out.
- The Belgrade Declaration of Non-Aligned Countries (1961): no copy could be reached (an Internet Archive item was the wrong book; the PALMM copy answered HTTP 410).
- The 1954 India–China agreement (Five Principles): text found only in a database transcription, and its preamble alone was too short for a three-question set.
- The Warsaw Pact text on the Avalon Project: it reprints a 1955 New Times (Moscow) translation of uncertain status, so the UN Secretariat translation was used instead.
