# INDEX: AP World Unit 8 (Cold War and Decolonization, c. 1900–present) sources

Copyright rule for this unit: every text, image, and data set is public domain. Each is either (a) a work of the U.S. government (17 U.S.C. § 105): speeches and papers of U.S. officials, State Department and CIA documents printed in Foreign Relations of the United States (FRUS), a State Department translation of a Soviet letter, a U.S. Joint Publications Research Service (JPRS) translation of Mao, the Pentagon Papers, the 9/11 Commission Report, CIA and Census statistics, and U.S. Air Force photographs; or (b) published in 1930 or earlier (Pan-African Congress manifesto, 1921; Gandhi's trial statement, printed 1924). No post-1930 non-government text is quoted, and nothing is paraphrased as a quotation. Each file records its license basis.

Every text excerpt was copied from raw text downloaded with curl and saved in sources/raw/ (FRUS volumes as the Office of the Historian's TEI XML from GitHub, extracted with a throwaway script), then machine-checked against the raw text: every stretch between ellipses, brackets, or footnote marks had to occur in the raw text once both were reduced to letters and digits (FRUS editors' notes ignored). The stretches that did not match exactly were read by hand; each is an OCR slip (X5 "vhich," P1's decorated capital), a page header or column break inside a sentence (P3, P8), a typo in the Avalon transcription corrected from the Public Papers (X8 "it poses"), or a FRUS editors' bracketed correction (P12.1 "Amritsar"). Images were confirmed with the Commons API (raw/commons/*.json), with requests spaced several minutes apart because Wikimedia answers HTTP 429.
