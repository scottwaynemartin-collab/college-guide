## MUST-FIX

1. **U8-E20: two defensible answers (borderline).** The stem asks what principle 6(A) is "most closely associated with." Principle 6(A) was written with SEATO and the Baghdad Pact in mind, so a strong student can argue for C (SEATO). The item's own rationale for C concedes the link: "the kind of arrangement that principle 6(A) warns against." D is still the best answer, but the stem invites the dispute.
   **Fix:** change the stem to: "Principle 6(A) in section G best reflects the goals of which movement?" Leave the options, the key (D), and the rationales unchanged; they already explain why SEATO is the opposite.

2. **U8-P27 explanation: factual overstatement.** The explanation says: "Even the European power that held on longest could not stop independence." That is not strictly true. Spain kept Spanish Sahara until February 1976, after Angola became independent in November 1975. France kept Djibouti until 1977. Southern Rhodesia was legally a British colony until 1980.
   **Fix:** "Portugal fought to keep its African colonies long after most of Africa was independent, and even it could not stop independence, which fits Source 2's belief."
