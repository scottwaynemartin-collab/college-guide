03
The cross-unit reference

Ideas That Mutate

The -Isms Tracker

18 ideologies, 65 stages

Liberalism in 1830 is not liberalism in 1930. Nationalism unifies Italy and then wrecks Yugoslavia. Every -ism on this course is introduced in one unit and then changes shape for the rest of it — and that movement is what the free-response questions actually ask about.

Each entry gives the one-line definition, where it first appears, who held it, what it defined itself against, a stage-by-stage strip across the units, the mistake students reliably make, and what the exam tests.

Open the tracker
How to use this site
  • Studying a unit test. Open the unit, run the vocabulary in quiz mode, then check the timeline and the key concepts.
  • Writing a DBQ or LEQ. Start on the period page for context and the through-line, pull specifics from the unit pages, then practice in the Writing Room.
  • Stuck on an -ism. Go to the tracker and read the stage strip left to right rather than re-reading one unit.
  • Practising stimulus. Every unit has an art and stimulus section — describe the source before you interpret it.
