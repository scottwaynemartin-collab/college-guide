### Source P1 (questions U5-P01–U5-P03)
> Man is born free; and everywhere he is in chains. One thinks himself the master of others, and still remains a greater slave than they. How did this change come about? I do not know. What can make it legitimate? That question I think I can answer. …
> "The problem is to find a form of association which will defend and protect with the whole common force the person and goods of each associate, and in which each, while uniting himself with all, may still obey himself alone, and remain as free as before." This is the fundamental problem of which the Social Contract provides the solution. …
> "Each of us puts his person and all his power in common under the supreme direction of the general will, and, in our corporate capacity, we receive each member as an indivisible part of the whole."
— Jean-Jacques Rousseau, philosopher born in Geneva who lived mostly in France, The Social Contract, 1762 (Translated by G. D. H. Cole (1913); adapted)

**U5-P01** [Topic 5.1 · LO A · Skill 3.A]  Rousseau's main claim in the passage is that a government is legitimate only when it
- (A) rests on an agreement among the people and is guided by the general will
- (B) is led by a king whose right to rule comes directly from God
- (C) divides power among separate branches of government so that no single group rules alone
- (D) is strong enough to make the people obey the laws it passes
KEY: A. Explanation: Rousseau asks what makes power "legitimate," or rightful. His answer is a social contract. People agree to join together, and the "general will" of the whole people guides them.
  - why not B: Rousseau says rightful power comes from people joining together, not from God choosing a king.
  - why not C: Dividing power among branches was Montesquieu's idea. Rousseau puts each person under "the supreme direction of the general will."
  - why not D: Force alone does not make a government rightful. Rousseau says its authority must rest on the people's shared agreement and the general will.
  - hint: Read the last paragraph. Who puts their power "in common," and what directs it?

- (C) the Renaissance, when artists revived the styles of ancient Greece and Rome
- (D) the rise of Social Darwinism, which applied ideas about evolution to whole societies
KEY: B. Explanation: Rousseau wrote in the 1700s. Thinkers like him used reason to ask what makes a government fair. They no longer simply accepted the power of kings and tradition.
  - why not A: The Reformation began in the 1500s and was mainly about religion. Rousseau wrote in 1762 about government.
  - why not C: Renaissance thinkers also wrote about government. But Rousseau wrote in 1762, during the Enlightenment of the 1700s.
  - why not D: Social Darwinism appeared in the late 1800s, more than 100 years after Rousseau wrote.

> As we consider the various classes of which the State is composed, we are convinced that the laws of our empire which have wisely provided for the upper and middle classes, and have fixed with precision their rights and obligations, have not reached the same degree of success in relation to the peasants bound to the soil, who, either through ancient laws or custom, have been hereditarily subjected to the authority of the landlords. …

> As every individual, therefore, endeavours as much as he can, both to employ his capital in the support of domestic industry, and so to direct that industry that its produce may be of the greatest value; every individual necessarily labours to render the annual revenue of the society as great as he can.
