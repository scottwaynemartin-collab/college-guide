# INDEX: AP World Unit 6 (Consequences of Industrialization, c. 1750–1900) sources

Every text excerpt was copied from raw text downloaded with curl and saved in sources/raw/ (second scans in raw/alt/ or alongside), then machine-checked stretch by stretch against the raw text with a throwaway script: each stretch between ellipses, brackets, or footnote marks had to occur in the raw text after whitespace, line-end hyphens, and quote marks were normalized. Every stretch that did not match exactly was read by hand; each mismatch is an OCR slip, a running head or page break, a marginal heading, or an interleaved column (the Chinese text of the 1917 treaty volume), and each is listed in its file. Every edition or translation used was published in 1930 or earlier. Images are public-domain files on Wikimedia Commons, confirmed with the Commons API (spaced requests after HTTP 429 responses) and downloaded to raw/commons/ to write captions. Chart values were read from two scans (Ferenczi) or from the page image with every row summed against its printed total (Ellison).

Notes:
- Fall 2026 CED Unit 6 has eight topics (6.1–6.8) and Learning Objectives A–I (6.6 has F and G); see ced_unit6_excerpt.txt.
- No Unit 5 source is reused (Unit 5 used Dutt, Wells, St. John, Zhang Zhidong, Weber, and Bairoch).
- Considered and not used: Cecil Rhodes's 1877 "Confession of Faith" (Stead 1902 prints only fragments mixed with summary, and the Rhodes cartoon already covers Rhodes); Jules Ferry's 1884 speech (no pre-1930 English translation found); Congo rubber export series from Morel and Doyle (no clean year-by-year table found); Wingate's translations of the Mahdi (Xhosa and Ghost Dance chosen for prophetic resistance); Markham's History of Peru (Mulhall covers guano).
- Graphic or demeaning passages left out on purpose: Theal's famine and cannibalism scenes and his slur for the Xhosa (X6); the Taiping proclamation's "fox and dog" and "dogs and swine" passages (P5); the Bareilly proclamation's calls to kill (P4); Gandhi's sentences repeating planters' stereotypes of African workers (X8). Kipling's and Pearson's racist wording is kept because the questions depend on it, with footnotes that label it.
