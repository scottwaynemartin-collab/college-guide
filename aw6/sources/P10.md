ADAPTATION IN THE BANK (every cut):
The bracket [Buenos Aires] glosses 'B. Ayres'; the bracket [In Peru] supplies the country heading. Cuts: after 'shape of loans.' (Wheelwright's Chilean line; the sentence on the first Argentine line, which Mulhall dates 1853 although it opened in 1857, so it is cut; the line's later extension and the table of mileage); after 'English company' (passenger figures); in the Peru paragraph: the 1822–25 loans sentence is kept to 'contracted in London' (the rates of issue and the total are cut and marked) so that 'No interest was paid from 1825' has its referent (a footnote notes that Peruvians had long used guano as fertilizer and that what was new around 1840 was its sale to Europe, since Mulhall's 'discovery … (1842)' could mislead); Osma's 1849 settlement, the named lenders of the 1852 loans, the Arica railway loan, the deposits' tonnage, and the 1872 loan are cut and marked.

CONTEXT NOTES:
Mulhall and his brother founded The Standard (Buenos Aires), an English-language daily, in 1861. Peru defaulted in 1876; the Grace Contract (1889) handed its railways to British bondholders.
