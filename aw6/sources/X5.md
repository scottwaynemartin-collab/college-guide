OCR / TEXT NOTES:
Column heads read from the page image: United States | Brazil | Egypt | Turkey &c. | W. Indies &c. | East Indies | China & Japan | Total. Rows used (thousands of bales): 1860: 4,058 | 48 | 135 | 21 | 23 | 552 | — | total 4,837. 1864: 241 | 127 | 427 | 188 | 39 | 1,374 | 239 | total 2,635. Each row sums exactly to its printed total. Ellison's text confirms the figures: 'the United States furnished 4,058,000 bales' in 1860; 'The Brazilian supply rose from 48,000 bales in 1860'; the import from India 'failed to raise the import into Europe to more than 1,374,000 bales' (1864). For E16, the 1870 row (United States 2,345; East Indies 1,057; total 4,213) shows the U.S. again the largest source.

ADAPTATION IN THE BANK (every cut):
Only four columns and two years are charted (the app draws at most two series). The chart note says the table covers all of Europe, in bales of 400 lb, that 'East Indies' is shown as 'India', and that smaller sources are left out.

CONTEXT NOTES:
The Union blockade (from 1861) and the Confederacy's early embargo cut U.S. exports; Lancashire's 'cotton famine' followed, and Indian and Egyptian cotton expanded. Egypt's cotton boom fed Khedive Ismail's borrowing.
