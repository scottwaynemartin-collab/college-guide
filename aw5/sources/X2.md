ADAPTATION IN THE BANK:
- Four short cuts from the long proclamation, marked with ellipses. Left out: the passages calling for vengeance on the French and describing killings of children, which are graphic and not needed for the questions.
- Footnote added: "barbarians" = the French.

CONTEXT NOTES:
- Napoleon sent General Leclerc in 1802 to retake Saint-Domingue; Toussaint Louverture was seized in June 1802 and died in France in 1803; France restored slavery in Guadeloupe in 1802.
- The French surrendered at Le Cap in November 1803; Haiti declared independence on 1 January 1804 at Gonaïves.
- The paragraph promising not to be 'revolutionary fire-brands' was addressed to neighbouring slaveholding colonies (British Jamaica, Spanish Cuba).
- Haiti later aided Bolívar (1815–1816) on condition he free the enslaved.
