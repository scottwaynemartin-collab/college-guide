Check of sums: 32.8 + 24.5 + 3.8 = 61.1 (1750) and 19.7 + 8.6 + 2.6 = 30.9 (1860), matching Bergesen's figures.

Label: Bairoch's row is the India–Pakistan region (the whole subcontinent, including present-day Bangladesh), not all of South Asia; Bergesen's summary (raw/wsarch_ucr_0118.html) groups it as "India/Pakistan." The chart now labels it "India–Pakistan" and the note says it covers today's India, Pakistan, and Bangladesh. The reviewer's "Table 10, p. 296" locator could not be confirmed here, so the citation gives only the journal, volume 11, and year.

Values used in the chart (1750 → 1900): China 32.8 → 6.2; India–Pakistan 24.5 → 1.7; Japan 3.8 → 2.4; Russia 5.0 → 8.8; United Kingdom 1.9 → 18.5; United States 0.1 → 23.6.
Chart notes: two series only (the page renderer styles two series). Bairoch's figures are estimates; shares are relative, and world output grew many times over, so a falling share does not by itself mean a region made fewer goods (no question depends on this).
