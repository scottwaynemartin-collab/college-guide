CONTEXT NOTES:
- Adopted 26 August 1789, two months after the Estates-General became the National Assembly and six weeks after the fall of the Bastille.
- Article 6's "general will" echoes Rousseau; article 16's separation of powers echoes Montesquieu; article 10's protection of religious opinion reflects Voltaire and the toleration debates.
- Free people of color from Saint-Domingue lobbied the Assembly in 1789–1790 using its language; the enslaved uprising began in August 1791.
- Olympe de Gouges answered with the Declaration of the Rights of Woman and of the Female Citizen (1791). No pre-1930 English translation of de Gouges was found, so she appears only as an answer choice.
