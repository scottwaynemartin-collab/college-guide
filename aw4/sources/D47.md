EXCERPT (verbatim):
[T]hay saied thay had bine the first in doing good to the English, and the English the first in doing rong, saied when the English first Came their kings father was as a great man and the English as a litell Child, he Constraened other indians from ronging [OCR in one scan: "rouging"] the English and gave them Coren and shewed them how to plant and was free to do them ani good and had let them have a 100 times more land, then now the king had for his own peopell, but ther kings brother when he was king Came miserabely to dy by being forsed to Court as thay judged poysoned, and another greavanc was if 20 of there onest indians testefied that a Englishman had dun [OCR in one scan: "dim"] them rong, it was as nothing, and if but one of ther worst indians testefied against ani indian or ther king when it plesed the English that was sufitiant. a nother grivanc was when ther kings sold land the English wold say it was more than thay agred to and a writing must be prove against all them, [...] now home the English had owned for king or queen thay wold disinheret, and make a nother king that wold give or seell them there land, that now thay had no hopes left to kepe ani land, a nother grivanc the English Catell and horses still incresed that when thay removed 30 mill from wher English had anithing to do, thay Could not kepe ther coren from being spoyled, thay never being iused to fence, and thoft when the English boft land of them that thay wold have kept ther Catell upone ther owne land, a nother grevanc the English wear so eger to sell the indians lickers that most of the indians spent all in drunknes and then ravened upone the sober indians and thay did belive often did hurt the English Catell, and ther kings Could not prevent it.

(Editorial marks: [...] omits a passage saying some sachems had sold so much land that their people had none, that the English made some sachems drunk and cheated them in bargains, and that sachems were now warned not to sell land for far less than its value. [T] = capital supplied at the start of the excerpt (mid-sentence in the original: "...therfore we Consented to here them, thay saied..."). Lincoln's footnote numbers removed. Easton's own spelling and lack of capitals are original, not OCR errors. The two scans differ on one capital ("onest Indians"/"onest indians"); lowercase used to match Easton's usage elsewhere.)

CONTEXT NOTES:
- Speakers: "thay" = Metacom ("King Philip"), sachem of the Wampanoag (Pokanoket), and his councilors, speaking to Easton and other Rhode Island magistrates about a week before fighting began in June 1675. "Their kings father" = Massasoit, who allied with the Plymouth colonists in 1621; "ther kings brother" = Wamsutta (Alexander), who died in 1662 soon after being forced to appear before Plymouth authorities ("forsed to Court").
- Glossary (from Lincoln's notes and context): rong = wrong; Coren = corn; prove = proof; home = whom; "thay wold disinheret" = the English would depose; thoft = thought; boft = bought; mill = miles; lickers = liquors; ravened upone = preyed upon.
- The grievances: unequal treatment in colonial courts, disputed land sales and deeds, English interference in choosing Native leaders, free-ranging English livestock destroying unfenced Native cornfields, and the alcohol trade.
- Outcome: King Philip's War (1675-1676) was one of the deadliest colonial wars in proportion to population; Metacom was killed in August 1676, and many captured Native people were sold into slavery in the West Indies.
- Sourcing: Easton was a Quaker and a Rhode Islander; Rhode Island distrusted Plymouth and Massachusetts, and Puritan writer Increase Mather accused Easton of favoring the Indians. Easton's report is secondhand (his summary of what the Wampanoag said).
