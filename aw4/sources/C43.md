EXCERPT (verbatim):
PART A - Rander, a Muslim port town of Gujarat (Vol. I, Section 59)
Coming forth from this town of Limadura, and returning to those with havens on the sea, and going beyond Guindarim further along the coast, there is a river on the hither side of which stands a Moorish town named Reynel, wherein are very fair houses and open places. It is a very pleasant and wealthy place, for the Moorish inhabitants thereof trade in their own ships with Malaca, Benguala, Camarasym and Peguu, also to Martabam and Çamatra, in spices of divers sorts and drugs and silks in great abundance, musk, benzoin, porcelain and many other wares. Those who dwell here have many great and fair vessels, which carry on this trade, and whosoever would have at his disposal things from Malaca and China, let him go to this place, where he will find them in greater perfection than in any other place soever. The Moors who dwell here are wealthy and distinguished, fair in colour and of gentle birth. They go well attired, their women are beautiful and they have good houses well kept and furnished. They use, in the front room of their houses, to have many shelves all round, the whole room being surrounded by them as in a shop, all filled with fair and rich porcelain of new styles. [...]

PART B - Malacca (Vol. II)
And here dwell up to now great wholesale merchants of every kind, both Moors and Heathen, many of them from Charamandel, men of great estates and owning many great ships which they call juncos. They trade everywhere in goods of all kinds. [...] they go to Tanaçary, Peeguu, Bengala, Paleacate, Charamandel, Malabar and Cambaya, so much so that this city of Malaca is the richest seaport with the greatest number of wholesale merchants and abundance of shipping and trade that can be found in the whole world.

CONTEXT NOTES:
- Barbosa served the Portuguese in Cannanore and Cochin c. 1500-1516 and spoke Malayalam; his book describes Indian Ocean ports from East Africa to China as they worked in the first years after Portuguese arrival. He died on Magellan's expedition in 1521.
- Reynel = Rander, on the Tapti River opposite Surat in Gujarat (the "Kingdom of Cambaya"); its "Moors" were Muslim (Navayat) merchant families. Place names: Malaca = Malacca (Melaka, Malay Peninsula), Benguala = Bengal, Camarasym/Tanaçary = Tenasserim (Burma/Thailand coast), Peguu = Pegu (Burma), Martabam = Martaban, Çamatra = Sumatra, Charamandel = Coromandel Coast (SE India), Paleacate = Pulicat, Cambaya = Gujarat/Cambay. "Heathen" = Hindus (and other non-Muslims); "juncos" = junks.
- Portugal captured Malacca in 1511, a few years before Barbosa finished; "up to now" signals that Asian merchants still traded there. The Portuguese burned Rander and Surat in 1530.
- Use for continuity: Gujarati, Tamil/Chettiar, Malay, Javanese and Chinese merchants and Muslim shipping networks predated the Portuguese and kept operating alongside them (CED KC-4.3.II.A.iii).
