EXCERPT (verbatim):
But the force of silver, which drawes vnto it the desire of all thinges, hath peopled this mountaine more then any other place in all these Kingdomes, making it so fruitfull of all kindes of meats, as there wantes nothing that can be desired, yea, in great aboundance [OCR: "abouiidance"] [...].

Finally, at this day the King receives for his fift yeerely, from the mines of Potosi, a million of silver, besides the wealth that growes by quicksilver [source breaks the word across a line: "quick / silver"] and other royall prerogatives, which is a great treasure. [...] In that fleete where I came in the yeare one thousand five hundred eighty seaven [OCR: "seaveu"], there were eleven millions transported in the two fleets of Peru and Mexico, whereof two thirds were in that of Peru, and almost the one halfe [OCR: "halfo"] for the King. I thought good to set this downe particularly, to shew the power which his heavenly Maiestie hath given to the Kings of Spaine, heaping so many Crowns and Kingdomes [OCR: "Kingdonies"] vpon them, who (by the especiall favour of heaven) have ioyned the East with the West Indies, invironing the whole worlde with their Power. [...]

They labour in these mines in continuall [OCR: "continual!"] darknes and obscuritie, without knowledge of day or night. [...] Those that labour therein vse candles to light them, dividing their work in such sort, as they that worke in the day rest by the night, and so they change. [...] After, they carry vp this mettall upon their shoulders, by ladders [...]. A man carries ordinarily the weight of two arrobas of mettall vpon his shoulders, tied together in a cloth in maner of a skippe, and so mount they three and three. He that goes before carries a candle tied to his thumb, [...] and so go they vp the ladder holding it with both their handes; to mount so great a height which commonly is above a hundred and fiftie estados [OCR: "cstados"], a fearful thing and which breeds an amazement to thinke vpon it, so great is the desire of silver, that for the gaine thereof men indure any paines.

CONTEXT NOTES:
- Potosí (in today's Bolivia; then the Province of Charcas, Viceroyalty of Peru) was registered as a silver mine in April 1545. Acosta reports that by c. 1585 it had produced enormous sums, and that the Crown's "fift" (the royal fifth, the quinto, a 20% tax on mined silver) brought the king about a million pesos a year.
- "A million of silver" / "eleven millions" = pesos (pieces of eight), not pounds or tons. Acosta sailed home with the 1587 treasure fleets. "Ioyned the East with the West Indies" refers to Spain's Philippine colony (Manila) and Philip II's union with Portugal (1580), which linked American silver to Asian trade.
- An "arroba" = about 25 lb (so two arrobas = about 50 lb, Markham's note); an "estado" = the height of a man (roughly 1.7 m), so 150 estados is roughly 250 m of ladders. A "skippe" = a basket.
- Acosta does not name the labor system in these chapters, but the workers he describes were overwhelmingly Indigenous Andeans, many conscripted through the mita, a rotating forced-labor draft adapted from the Inca mit'a and reorganized for Potosí by Viceroy Francisco de Toledo in the 1570s (about one-seventh of adult men from designated provinces served in turns). The excerpt's "they that worke in the day rest by the night, and so they change" describes shifts within the mine, not the mita rotation itself.
- No passage in Grimston's Book IV explicitly describes the mita draft; the target "brought by turns" detail is not in this translation (searched the full volume for "mita," "turnes," "by course").
