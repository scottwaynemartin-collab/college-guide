EXCERPT (verbatim):
The discovery of gold and silver in America, the extirpation, enslavement and entombment in mines of the aboriginal population, the beginning of the conquest and looting of the East Indies, the turning of Africa into a warren for the commercial hunting of black-skins, signalised the rosy dawn of the era of capitalist production. These idyllic proceedings are the chief momenta of primitive accumulation. On their heels treads the commercial war of the European nations, with the globe for a theatre. It begins with the revolt of the Netherlands from Spain, assumes giant dimensions in England's anti-jacobin war, and is still going on in the opium wars against China, &c.

The different momenta of primitive accumulation distribute themselves now, more or less in chronological order, particularly over Spain, Portugal, Holland, France, and England. In England at the end of the 17th century, they arrive at a systematical combination, embracing the colonies, the national debt, the modern mode of taxation, and the protectionist system. These methods depend in part on brute force, e.g., the colonial system. But they all employ the power of the State, the concentrated and organised force of society, to hasten, hothouse fashion, the process of transformation of the feudal mode of production into the capitalist mode, and to shorten the transition. Force is the midwife of every old society pregnant with a new one. It is itself an economic power.

CONTEXT NOTES:
- Marx argued that capitalism did not arise peacefully from thrift; its starting capital ("primitive accumulation") came from force: colonial plunder, coerced labor in American mines, the Atlantic slave trade, and state power (colonies, public debt, taxation, protective tariffs). This is a 19th-century interpretation written by a critic of capitalism, not an eyewitness account of 1450-1750.
- Wording warning: "the commercial hunting of black-skins" is the 1887 translators' rendering of Marx's sarcastic phrase for the slave trade (German "Handelsjagd auf Schwarzhäute"); it is offensive by modern standards and should be framed or trimmed for students. "Idyllic" and "rosy dawn" are ironic. "Momenta" = driving moments/factors. "Entombment in mines" refers to forced labor (e.g., Potosí silver mines and the mita system), not a burial practice.
- Events named: Dutch Revolt against Spain (1568-1648); "England's anti-jacobin war" = Britain's wars against Revolutionary/Napoleonic France (1793-1815); Opium Wars (1839-42, 1856-60). These last two fall after 1750.
- Pairs well with C48 (Smith 1776: the discoveries were good for mankind overall but disastrous for natives) and C412 (Hamilton's data on American treasure imports to Spain).
