EXCERPT (verbatim):
PART A - Arrival at Calicut, May 1498
On the following day [May 21] these same boats came again alongside, when the captain-major sent one of the convicts to Calecut, and those with whom he went took him to two Moors from Tunis, who could speak Castilian and Genoese. The first greeting that he received was in these words: "May the Devil take thee! What brought you hither?" They asked what he sought so far away from home, and he told them that we came in search of Christians and of spices. [...]

PART B - The gifts for the ruler of Calicut
On Tuesday [May 29] the captain got ready the following things to be sent to the king, viz., twelve pieces of lambel, four scarlet hoods, six hats, four strings of coral, a case containing six wash-hand basins, a case of sugar, two casks of oil, and two of honey. And as it is the custom not to send anything to the king without the knowledge of the Moor, his factor, and of the bale, the captain informed them of his intention. They came, and when they saw the present they laughed at it, saying that it was not a thing to offer to a king, that the poorest merchant from Mecca, or any other part of India, gave more, and that if he wanted to make a present it should be in gold, as the king would not accept such things. When the captain heard this he grew sad, and said that he had brought no gold, that, moreover, he was no merchant, but an ambassador; that he gave of that which he had, which was his own [private gift] and not the king's; that if the King of Portugal ordered him to return he would intrust him with far richer presents; and that if King Camolim would not accept these things he would send them back to the ships. [...] When they had gone there came certain Moorish merchants, and they all depreciated the present which the captain desired to be sent to the king.

CONTEXT NOTES:
- Vasco da Gama's fleet left Lisbon in July 1497, rounded the Cape of Good Hope, picked up a pilot at Malindi (East Africa), and reached the Malabar Coast near Calicut (Kozhikode) on 20 May 1498. The "king" is the Zamorin (Samudri raja) of Calicut, a Hindu ruler; "Camolim" is the journal's spelling of that title.
- "Moors" = Muslims; here North African and Arab/Indian Muslim merchants who already dominated Calicut's pepper and spice trade with the Red Sea and Mecca. "Factor" = commercial agent; "bale" = a local official (governor). "Lambel" = striped cloth (Ravenstein's note). The "convict" (degredado) was a condemned man carried to do risky landings.
- Misreading risk: the Portuguese believed Calicut's Hindus were "Christians" (the journal says the city "is inhabited by Christians"); this was a mistake. Ravenstein's own note says Gama "was very poorly provided with suitable merchandise," which explains the scene.
- What happened next: Gama obtained a cargo of pepper and spices with difficulty and returned to Lisbon in 1499; the profit encouraged larger armed fleets (Cabral 1500; Gama again 1502), Portuguese forts at Goa (1510) and Malacca (1511), and attempts to control Indian Ocean trade by force (the cartaz pass system).
