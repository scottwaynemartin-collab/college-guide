EXCERPT (verbatim):
America was discovered and found out Ann. Dom. 1492, and the Year insuing inhabited by the Spaniards [...]. Their first attempt was on the Spanish Island, which indeed is a most fertile soil [...]: Nay it is on all sides surrounded with an almost innumerable number of Islands, which we found so well peopled with Natives and Forreigners, that there is scarce any Region in the Universe fortified with so many Inhabitants [...].

[...] of Three Millions of Persons, which lived in Hispaniola itself, there is at present but the inconsiderable remnant of scarce Three Hundred. [...]

Now the ultimate end and scope that incited the Spaniards to endeavor the Extirptaion [sic] and Desolation of this People, was Gold only; that thereby growing opulent in a short time, they might arrive at once at such Degrees and Dignities, as were no wayes consistent with their Persons. [...]

The Warlike Engagements being over, and the Inhabitants all swept away, they divided among themselves the Young Men, Women, and Children reserved promiscuously for that purpose, one obtained thirty, another forty, to this Man one hundred were disposed, to the other two hundred, and the more one was in favor with the domineering Tyrant (which they styled Governor) the more he became Master of, upon this pretence, and with this Proviso, that he should see them instructed in the Catholick Religion, when as they themselves to whom they were committed to be taught, and the care of their Souls instructed them were, for the major part Idiots, Cruel, Avaritious, infected and stained with all sorts of Vices. And this was the great care they had of them, they sent the Males to the Mines to dig and bring away the Gold, which is an intollerable labor; but the Women they made use of to Manure and Till the ground [...]. The Men died in Mines, hunger starved and oppressed with labor, and the Women perished in the Fields, harrassed and broken with the like Evils and Calamities: Thus an infinite number of Inhabitants that formerly peopled this Island were exterminated and dwindled away to nothing by such Consumptions.

CONTEXT NOTES:
- Las Casas came to Hispaniola in 1502, held an encomienda himself, renounced it in 1514, and became a Dominican and the leading critic of Spanish treatment of Indigenous people. He wrote the "Brief Account" (1542) for Emperor Charles V; that year the New Laws restricted the encomienda (later partly revoked after settler protest). He debated Juan Ginés de Sepúlveda at Valladolid in 1550-51.
- The passage describes the encomienda: Indigenous people were "committed" (encomendados) to a Spaniard, who could demand their labor and tribute in return for a duty to Christianize them. "Spanish Island" = Hispaniola. "Promiscuously" here means "indiscriminately, without distinction"; "Idiots" means "unlearned men"; "Consumptions" means "wastings away."
- His population numbers (3 million on Hispaniola, fewer than 300 left) are rhetorical estimates that modern historians debate; the collapse was real, but most scholars stress epidemic disease (e.g., smallpox from 1518) together with forced labor, famine and violence.
- Sourcing point: this 1689 London edition was published by English Protestants as anti-Catholic, anti-Spanish propaganda (see the title "Popery Truly Display'd"), part of what later historians call the "Black Legend."
