# INDEX-H: Secondary-source (historians') excerpts for AP World Unit 4 practice exam

All works published 1904-1929 (US public domain). Every excerpt was taken from raw text downloaded with curl (archive.org djvu.txt or Project Gutenberg) and kept in raw/. Where a scan's OCR was messy, the wording was checked against a second scan of the same edition (files ending _verify.txt). Ellipses ( . . . ) mark cuts; all cuts and OCR fixes are listed in each file's "Editorial notes." Word counts exclude ellipses (hyphenated words count as one).

## Targets not reached / substitutions
- H41: Hamilton's 1929 Economica article WAS reachable (archive.org item economica_1929_9_25-27). His 1929 QJE article was not needed; his 1934 book is too late for the rule.
- H44: Used The Negro (1915); The Suppression of the African Slave-Trade (1896) is downloaded in raw/ but is about US law and policy, so it was not excerpted.
- H45: Neither Whiteway nor Jayne has a passage that names the "cartaz" outright; they call the passes "safe-conducts," "passes," and "licences." H45c's notes give Whiteway's verbatim safe-conduct sentence (pp. 173-174), and H45b mentions licences.
- H47 (food crops): not written as a file. A good verified option is in raw/: Berthold Laufer, Tobacco and Its Use in Asia (Field Museum Anthropology Leaflet 18, Chicago, 1924), pp. 2-3 and 17. It argues that tobacco, and with it the peanut and sweet potato, reached China through Fujian merchants trading in Spanish Manila, not through the Portuguese, and it traces "three movements of the tobacco plant into Asia." Alphonse de Candolle, Origin of Cultivated Plants (1884; Gutenberg #45917), is also in raw/, but it is mostly a technical discussion of plant origins.
- Clive Day, A History of Commerce (1907), is in raw/ but its Google-scan OCR is badly damaged; it was not used.

## Cautions for item writers
- Dated language appears in several excerpts and is listed in each CONTEXT NOTES section: "native stock," "Indian vassals" (H43); "nature peoples," "a negro on one of his ships" (H47); "Mohammedan," "bronze flesh" (H44); "Oriental" (H41b); "infidels" (H45, voicing Almeida's view); "Muhamedans" (H45c). None of them are slurs, but each needs a one-line gloss on the exam page.
- Some factual claims are outdated, and the notes give the modern view: Asante's rise is dated to about 1700, not 1719 (H44); Hamilton's "hoarding" explanation and his Europe-only silver route (H41b); Bourne's reliance on laws rather than enforcement (H43).
- Du Bois's very high death-toll estimates (60 to 100 million) come from the same chapter. They were deliberately NOT excerpted, because modern figures are about 12.5 million people embarked in the Atlantic trade.
